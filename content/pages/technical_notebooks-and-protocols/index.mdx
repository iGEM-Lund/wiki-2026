    Week 29 was dedicated to running colony PCRs to check multiple rounds of transformations, along with troubleshooting restriction digestion with NdeI and BamHI.   **Main results of the week:**

  Agarose gel electrophoresis of colony PCR products of the July 10th transformants showed that all colonies, covering every construct transformed on Friday, were negative for inserts. Considering this was not the first time colony PCR had failed, it raised concerns about the integrity of our restriction digestion. It’s possible the colonies were transformed with a religated pET-11a vector. 

  <FigureWithCaption />

  Agarose gel electrophoresis of restriction digestion performed on the same day confirmed our concerns. The NdeI control was almost identical to the undigested plasmid control. The double digested samples look identical to single digested ones, since the excised fragment is very small (\~40bp), making it impossible to distinguish single and double digested pET-11a on the gel. This is likely why most of our transformations have been unsuccessful. We decided to test the NdeI and BamHI aliquots we had in the freezer, in order to identify which ones would digest correctly.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-13-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-11a with NdeI and BamHI, analysed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Ladder used was Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder. Lanes are described on the figure." />

  Testing enzyme integrity gave different results than the previous gel. Both NdeI aliquots cut the plasmid, though the digestion was still incomplete. BamHI doesn't seem to be a problem, as it fully digested the plasmid. To avoid any further mistakes, we have decided to place an order for a new NdeI enzyme, and use it for further digestion.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-14-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-11a with NdeI and BamHI aliquotes to test enzyme integrity, analysed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Lanes: (1) NdeI aliquote 1, (2) NdeI aliquote 2, (3) undigested pET-11a, (4) BamHI aliquote 1, (5) BamHI aliquote 2." />

  Because the pET-11a cutout was very small after digestion, we decided to approach restriction digestion from a different angle. We have previously ordered ci2350-MBP synthesized in the pET-BLANK plasmid, which has NdeI and BamHI sites flanking the insert. Since the pET-BLANK cutout corresponds to the full ci2350-MBP insert (\~3000 bp), significantly larger than the pET-11a cutout, double digesting this plasmid would let us distinguish single from double-digested plasmid much more easily, allowing gel extraction of only the double-digested plasmid. Although this approach would not yield digested pET-11a, it would allow cloning directly into the pET-BLANK vector, which we had ordered with a T7 promoter. We adopted this method for further cloning, as it proved more reliable than digesting pET-11a directly.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-16-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-BLANK containing ci2350-MBP with NdeI and BamHI.   After visualising the SDS-PAGE of soluble fractions from ci2350-His and ci2350-MBP expression cultures, we found no visible overexpression of ci2350-His at 70kDa mark, while ci2350-MBP expression was visible at 102 kDa mark. Ci2350-His might have not been expressed due to 37ºC cultivation temperature, as ci2350 was cultivated at 25ºC in our reference paper, Arp et al (2025). For further cultivation, we decided to test lowering the expression temperature to 17ºC, to minimize inclusion body formation and allow for proper folding.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-22-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Colony PCR screening of AKR1D1-His and AKR1D1-SUMO colonies, with ligations at different vector:insert molar ratios. Lanes: (1,12) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2-6) colonies from AKR1D1-His ligation at 1:1 vector:insert, (7-11) colonies from AKR1D1-His ligation at 1:3 vector:insert, (13-17) colonies from AKR1D1-SUMO ligation at 1:1 vector:insert, (18-22) colonies from AKR1D1-SUMO at 1:3 vector:insert, (23) negative PCR control." />

  To avoid restarting the whole transformation experiment from start (including plasmid miniprep and related steps), we decided to redo the digestion of already digested pET-11a, by incubating it again with NdeI, followed by another ligation reaction using only AKR1D1-His insert, to avoid wasting other inserts. Another round of transformation into *E. coli* TG1 was performed using this construct. Colony PCR showed that some colonies were positive, and sequencing identified a correct AKR1D1-His sequence.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-23-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Colony PCR screening of AKR1D1-His colonies, with ligations at different vector:insert molar ratios. Lanes: (1,8,14) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2-7, 9-13, 15,19) AKR1D1-His colonies, (20) negative PCR control." />

  Following our supervisor Camille's suggestion, we decided to digest pET-11a with NdeI only, allowing us to distinguish between single-digested and non-digested plasmid and extract it from the gel more easily. BamHI digestion, which is more reliable, would then follow separately. After visualizing the NdeI digestion products, the gel appeared very grainy, probably due to the agarose not fully dissolving in the microwave.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-23-gel2-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-BLANK containing ci2350-MBP with NdeI and BamHI. Lanes: (1,15) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2) NdeI control, (3) BamHI control, (4-14, 16-20) double digested samples." />

  To follow up on last week’s idea, we decided to digest pET-BLANK containing ci2350-MBP with NdeI and BamHI, to distinguish between single and double-digested plasmid, and extract only the double digested band from the gel, preventing religation of single-cut plasmid and false-positive transformations. Agarose gel electrophoresis of the pET-BLANK digestion gave some positive results, with a few bands being good candidates for gel extraction. However, the gel also showed a trend in which digestion strength decreased steadily across the well, from heavily digested plasmid to undigested plasmid. The most likely explanation is that restriction digestion mastermix was not properly mixed before aliquoting, where the first aliquots taken contained the most enzyme, while later ones contained progressively less, until some aliquots had essentially no enzyme at all, explaining the undigested wells. Going forward, we will ensure the mastermix is fully mixed before aliquoting, to prevent this from happening again.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-24-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-BLANK containing ci2350-MBP with NdeI and BamHI. Lanes: (1,14) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2) BamHI control, (3) NdeI control, (4) undigested sample, (5-13, 15-24) double digested sample." />

  To confirm that we had expressed ci2350-MBP, we analysed the SDS-PAGE gel to determine the molecular weight (Mr) of the overexpressed protein. Migration distances of Amersham ECL Rainbow marker bands were measured and used to contruct a standard curve of log^10(Mr) against relative mobility, from which the molecular weight of the overexpressed band was obtained. The results showed a molecular weight of \~113 kDa, close to the expected \~111 kDa of ci2350-MBP, confirming we had successfully expressed our desired protein.
