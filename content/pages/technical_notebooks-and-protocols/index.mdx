<Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
  <ExpandableTitle>
    Week 25
  </ExpandableTitle>

  <ExpandableSummary>
    **Weekly Summary:**

    During the first week of the lab we received a lab equipment introduction  from Johan, where we learned how to operate equipment properly and safely. Many consumables were made, including agar petri dishes, sterilized tips, Eppendorf tubes and MilliQ-H2O. Plasmid miniprep was done on *E. coli* TG1 cells containing pET-11a, followed by restriction digestion using BamHI and NdeI enzymes. Additionally, we prepared competent *E. coli* TG1 cells for future transformations.
  </ExpandableSummary>

  **Main results of the week:**

  Plasmid Miniprep of *E. coli* TG1 cells containing pET-11a. Jenna Bioscience Plasmid Miniprep Kit instructions state how the cells could be resuspended in Lysis buffer by pipetting or vortexing, and we decided to test if there was a difference. By looking at the results, non-vortexed samples had higher DNA concentration, and resuspension by pipetting was done in future experiments. Samples were pooled, allowing for more convenient downstream applications. A260/280 were slightly higher than expected, but we still decided to use these samples for restriction digestion.

  | Non-vortexed sample 2                  | 487                       | 2.081           | 1.972       |

  | Vortexed sample 4                      | 251                       | 2.053           | 1.996       |
  | Pooled sample                          | 347                       | 2.068           | 2.164       |

  Agarose gel visualisation of restriction digestion was done, without the holder for the gel. The ladder taken from last year's iGEM Team (New England Biolabs 1kb Plus Ladder DNA Ladder) was overloaded, and bands of digested pET-11a are slightly faint. In the future experiments, newly bought Ampliqon High Range DNA ladder can be used, along with keeping the gel in the holder.

Their concentration was too low to be used for transformations, so restriction digestion will be repeated next week, with prolonged incubation time.

  <ExpandableSummary>
    **Weekly summary:**

    The second week of the lab was dedicated to repeating and troubleshooting restriction digestion of pET-11a with BamHI and NdeI. Different protocols were tested, with different incubations times. Additionally, another plasmid miniprep kit was tested, which resulted in purer samples. Digestion of ci2350-His was done, followed by ligation and successful transformation into *E. coli* TG1.   **Main results of the week:**

  E.Z.N.A. plasmid miniprep kit was used as an alternative to Jena Bioscience kit, resulting in less concentration with higher purity. After consulting with our PI Johan, we concluded that samples prepared with Jena Bioscience Kit were contaminated with RNA, due to absorbance ratio 260/280 being \~2.0, and the concentration of nucleic acids being a lot higher than our PI usually gets. For future experiments, E.Z.N.A. plasmid miniprep kit was used.

  | S2                       | 92.4                      | 1.824           | 1.979            |
  | S3                       | 75.5                      | 1.831           | 1.810            |
  | S4 (column equilibrated) | 93.9                      | 1.853           | 2.305            |
  | S5 (column equilibrated) | 95.9                      | 1.804           | 2.474            |
  | S6 (column equilibrated) | 112                       | 1.852           | 2.306            |

  Restriction digestion with BamHI and NdeI of plasmid extracted with E.Z.N.A. plasmid miniprep kit showed noticeably stronger bands on agarose gel electrophoresis. This supports our assumption that earlier plasmid samples were significantly RNA contaminated. When we thought we were quantifying plasmid concentration, it was mostly RNA, leaving only a small fraction of DNA for digestion. This would explain both the faint bands in earlier digestions and the low plasmid yields after gel extraction.

  <ExpandableTitle>
    Week 27 (pdf link missing)
  </ExpandableTitle>

  <ExpandableSummary>
    **Weekly summary:**

    During Week 27, we focused mainly on transforming *E. coli* TG1 with our remaining gene constructs. Multiple rounds of transformations were required, as some attempts were unsuccessful, along with colony PCRs to confirm the presence of our inserts. Positive ci2350-His colonies were sequenced, and one colony with correct sequence was identified and stored for subsequent transformation into *E. coli* BL21.
  </ExpandableSummary>

  **Main results of the week:**

  Agarose gel electrophoresis of colony PCR products showed positive results in wells 2-9, corresponding to the expected size of the amplified ci2350-His insert region, confirming successful transformation of *E. coli* TG1 with ci2350-His. Multiple controls were tested, including colonies transformed with empty pET-11a, and colonies from a ligation control (ligation without insert). All controls proved negative, indicating the ligation and transformation were correct. Following our PI Johan’s recommendation, in the future transformations we will screen only 1-2 colonies from control plates and prioritize testing more transformant, to increase the chance of identifying a correctly transformed colony. Four ci2350-His positive colonies were sent for sequencing, where one was identified as having a correct construct.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/06-29-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Colony PCR screening of ci2350-His ligation and transformation into E. coli TG1. Lanes: (1,10,14,15) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2-5) colonies from plate 1, (6-9) colonies from plate 2, (11-13) colonies from empty pET-11a transformation, (16-18) colonies from ligation negative control, (19) PCR control." />

  By visualizing restriction digestion products of our ordered inserts and comparing the resulting fragment sizes, we confirmed the inserts were correct. Insert AKR1D1-MBP showed some additional bands, possibly due to too long digestion incubation causing non-specific cutting.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/06-30-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of inserts with NdeI and BamHI, analysed using agarose gel electrophoresis. Lanes: (1,5) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2) AKR1D1-His, (3) AKR1D1-TrxA, (4) AKR1D1-SUMO, (6) AKR1D1-MBP, (7) ci2350-TrxA, (8) ci2350-SUMO." />

  Transformation results were poorer than expected. All plates had very few colonies (\<7), suggesting that something went wrong, possibly during digestion or ligation. Plates containing AKR1D1-Trx and ci2350-Trx had no colonies. Agarose gel electrophoresis of colony PCR products showed only one positive colony (well 9, containing the ci2350-SUMO insert), with all other colonies tested negative. However, the amplified band in well 9 was smeary, which may have resulted from accidentally piercing the well while loading the sample. Since all other colonies were negative, we decided to repeat the transformations using new ligations prepared with the leftover digested plasmid from Week 26.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-02-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Colony PCR screening of insert transformation into E. coli TG1. Lanes: (1,8,15,16,23) Ampliqon High Range DNA Ladder, (2) ci2350 PCR control, (3) AKR1D1 PCR control, (4,5) colonies from negative ligation control, (6,7) colonies from empty pET-11a transformation, (9-11) ci2350-SUMO colonies, (12-14) AKR1D1-His colonies, (17-19) AKR1D1-SUMO colonies, (20-22) AKR1D1-MBP colonies." />

  Because plates with AKR1D1-Trx and ci2350-Trx inserts had no colonies, they were transformed using leftover ligation mix, and resulted in a few colonies, which were tested by colony PCR next week.

  <ExpandableTitle>
    Week 28
  </ExpandableTitle>

  <ExpandableSummary>
    **Weekly summary:**

    Week 28 focused on troubleshooting restriction digestion of pET-11a with NdeI and BamHI enzymes, and on redoing transformations of *E. coli* TG1 with our inserts. To reduce loss of digested plasmid during gel electrophoresis and extraction, we tested a PCR cleanup kit. Based on our lab supervisor Camille, we developed a new digestion protocol aimed at shortening the reaction time. Sequencing of positive transformations was carried out to check insert integrity, but unfortunately it revealed mutations. We received a safety introduction for handling testosterone, as it is a CMR reagent requiring special safety precautions.
  </ExpandableSummary>

  **Main results of the week:**

  After visualising restriction digestion of pET-11a on Monday, we found that the NdeI control was invalid, due to adding BamHI by mistake. The gel was discarded, since we were unsure whether to proceed, due to compromised control. Digestion was redone the next day, where the controls were set up correctly.

  <FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/notebooks-and-protocols/07-06-gel-named.avif" caption="Figure: Restriction digestion of pET-11a with NdeI and BamHI, analysed by agarose gel electrophoresis.   Restriction digestion of pET-11a was also carried out on Tuesday, followed by gel extraction and NanoDrop readings. The absorbance ratios were interesting to say the least. Nucleic acid concentrations were really low, and A260/280 were unusually high. The samples were discarded, and the digestion was repeated in the following days, using a PCR cleanup kit instead of gel extraction. Camille suggested this, as the yields are usually higher than gel extraction.
