## Questions we leave behind

<Card refId="card-qrvm3">
  <CardTitle>
    **How much testosterone circulates through the gut?**
  </CardTitle>

  This question came up repeatedly in our interviews, and none of the experts we have spoken with could provide a clear answer. <Ref target="card-vif4x" linkText="Angelica Lindén Hirschberg" /> confirmed that the gut is a major route of androgen elimination and that androgens undergo enterohepatic circulation, but could not say how much. <Ref target="card-8mtim" linkText="Anna Benrick" /> raised the same uncertainty. 

  This topic still remains unresolved in endocrinology, and it directly limits our project. Without that number, we cannot claim whether our gut-localized therapeutic could achieve a biologically meaningful effect, since we have no clear indication of how much testosterone is available in the gut for a 5β-reduction.
</Card>

<Card refId="card-qrvm3">
  <CardTitle>
    **Can a 5β-reductase be engineered to be steroid-selective?**
  </CardTitle>

  One of the major limitations we have encountered in our project is that 5β-reductases are not steroid specific, and engineering them to improve selectivity would require great effort. During our interview with <Ref target="card-ttroq" linkText="Trevor M. Penning" />, he explained the reason behind it: any substrate with a Δ4-3-ketosteroid structure is a plausible 5β-reduction substrate, and achieving single-steroid specificity would require multiple point mutations across the binding pocket rather than a single one. He also cautioned that this would be an extremely difficult task within the timeline of the project.

  We performed rational mutagenesis and molecular docking to identify potential ci2350 mutations to improve its selectivity for testosterone. Unfortunately, we were not able to test them experimentally. Whether selectivity on Δ4-3-ketosteroid reductases can be achieved remains an open question, and the mutations we have designed could serve as a starting point.
</Card>

<Card refId="card-qrvm3">
  <CardTitle>
    **Is an engineered probiotic safe for consumption by pregnant women?**
  </CardTitle>

  Following advice from <Ref target="card-vif4x" linkText="Angelica Lindén Hirschberg" />, we excluded pregnancy from our project scope. The advice was to avoid a live bacterial therapeutic during pregnancy. Because our therapy is aimed at people of reproductive age, any treatment targeting that population has to account for unplanned exposure.

  <Ref target="card-9nxm9" linkText="Mahnaz Bahri-Khomami" /> specified what would need to be established if the project were to move further: whether the therapeutic or its byproducts cross the placenta and whether 5β-reduction of androgens could affect male fetal development. Conventional probiotics are widely used during pregnancy, but the safety of genetically engineered probiotics has not yet been established.
</Card>
