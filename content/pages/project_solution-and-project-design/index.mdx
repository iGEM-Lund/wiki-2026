## Solution

Several possible effector strategies were considered for microbiome-based androgen modulation. One approach would be to inhibit 5α-microbial reductase, avoiding the conversion of testosterone in the highly androgenic metabolite dihydrotestosterone, or microbial β-glucuronidase or sulfatase activity, thereby reducing deconjugation of androgen metabolites and limiting the regeneration of free testosterone. However, these enzymes are intracellular and inhibiting their function is more complicated. For these reasons, a 5β-reductase-based strategy represents an attractive alternative for proof-of-concept development. 

The aim of this project is to develop and characterize a gut-gated synthetic biology system for the controlled expression of a steroid-modulating effector under gut-like conditions. By targeting androgen metabolism at the intestinal level, the proposed platform aims to provide a spatially localized approach to androgen attenuation. While substantial work would be required before any translational application, including optimization of enzyme choice, chassis selection, biocontainment, and in vivo validation, this project establishes an initial framework for investigating whether engineered gut-localized steroid metabolism could represent a complementary strategy for PMOS-associated hyperandrogenism.
