## Our Solution

### AnTigone: engineering controlled steroid transformation

AnTigone explores whether engineered bacteria could be used to modify androgenic steroids within the intestine. Building on the biological rationale presented in the [Background](https://wiki.igemlund.org/project_background), we propose a system that combines two functions: an enzyme capable of transforming steroid molecules and an expression system responsive to low-oxygen conditions.

Our central hypothesis is that **controlled expression of a steroid 5β-modulating enzymes could support the conversion of excess androgen into products with lower to none androgenic activity and/or lowering potent androgen bioavailability**.

### **Why steroid 5β-reduction?**

The biological activity of a steroid depends partly on its molecular structure. Steroid 5β-reductases modify the double bond between carbon atoms 4 and 5, changing the geometry of the steroid molecule. Human AKR1D1 provides an established example of this chemistry and can act on testosterone and other steroid substrates.&#x20;

<FigureWithCaption src="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/background/steroid-5-and-5-reduction.avif" scale={100} />

Our decision to investigate 5β-reduction was **informed by discussions** with researchers in microbiome biology and steroid metabolism. Liisa Veerus considered direct androgen biotransformation an experimentally tractable starting point because it provides measurable outputs in vitro. Trevor M. Penning supported the biochemical rationale for investigating AKR1D1-mediated testosterone conversion, while emphasizing the difficulty of achieving specificity for a single steroid. Also the reaction is one of the few that is a one way-direction and the products generated (...). Together, these information and discussions strengthened our decision to begin with a defined enzymatic reaction and assess both its activity and its limitations.

Elisabet Stener-Victorin further encouraged us to distinguish a useful proof-of-concept enzyme from a definitive therapeutic target. We therefore approach human AKR1D1 and microbial CI2350 5β-reductases as a tool for investigating the proposed mechanism, **without assuming that it would be the most suitable effector for a future clinical application**. The full consultation summaries are available on our [Human Practices page](https://wiki.igemlund.org/human-practices).

\
For AnTigone, this reaction offers a **defined starting point for engineering**. We can investigate enzyme production, substrate conversion and the biological activity of the resulting mixture as separate experimental questions.&#x20;

### **Connecting activity to environmental conditions**&#x20;

The second component of AnTigone is hypoxia-responsive expression. Our intended design uses low oxygen availability as an environmental input to regulate production of the steroid-transforming enzyme. Low oxygen is only one feature of the intended intestinal environment: a response to hypoxia would demonstrate environmental regulation, but would not by itself establish exclusive expression in the gut, relevant clinical situation or sufficient biological containment.

### **Designing for protein production and recovery**

A final product and a biochemical characterization require sufficient soluble protein. As we were aware of inclusion bodies and possible problems regarding human recombinant protein expression in a microbial system we decided to incorporate and test affinity and solubility tags, documented on our [Parts](https://wiki.igemlund.org/parts) page.

The construct collection includes a 6xHis affinity tag and fusion designs using maltose-binding protein (MBP), SUMO and thioredoxin (Trx). The chosen designs allows us to investigate if different fusion partners improve protein availability for downstream characterisation and applications. Construct sequences and architectures belong on the Parts page; experimental iterations and informed decisions are described in [Engineering](https://wiki.igemlund.org/engineering).
