## Results

### **Enzyme specificity engineering**

#### Overview

Two Δ4-3-ketosteroid 5β-reductases were taken forward for engineering: human **AKR1D1** and the bacterial enzyme **ci2350** from *Clostridium innocuum*. The aim was to shift substrate preference towards testosterone and away from progesterone, two steroids that differ only at the **C17 substituent**.

Structures were obtained from the PDB (AKR1D1, **3CAV**) and from **AlphaFold** (ci2350), and the functional pocket in each was located with **fpocket** and confirmed against **NCBI CD Search** annotations. Docking both substrates into the wild-type enzymes gave similar absolute affinities but opposite preferences: **AKR1D1 favoured progesterone by 0.5 kcal/mol,** while **ci2350 showed a marginal testosterone preference of 0.1 kcal/mol,** within the scoring noise.

Residues within **4 Å** of each docked ligand were sorted by which substrate they contacted. This identified three specificity determinants in AKR1D1 (**V309, L311, I57**) and six in ci2350 (**A107, R215, V263, V59, F71, Q100**). Substitutions at these positions were designed to weaken progesterone binding or strengthen testosterone binding, then screened for structural stability in **PyRosetta**. Of 24 AKR1D1 variants, 13 passed the **+5 REU** threshold; of 31 ci2350 variants, 17 passed. These variants were then docked with both substrates to assess their specificity towards testosterone.

The full workflow for both AKR1D1 and ci2350, from protein structure acquisition through to stability screening, is described on the [Modeling page](/modeling#enzyme-specificity-engineering-towards-testosterone).&#x20;

#### Docked poses of the AKR1D1 variants

Thirteen stable AKR1D1 variants were docked with both steroids. Selectivity is reported as ΔG(testosterone) − ΔG(progesterone), so a positive value means progesterone is bound more tightly; the wild type sits at +0.5 kcal/mol. A variant improves only if this value falls.

No variants shifted the preference. The largest gain was 0.1 kcal/mol, far below the ±0.5 kcal/mol noise of the scoring function, so V309S, V309E and I57Y cannot be called improvements. The only changes large enough to read as real went the wrong way: I57N moved the preference 0.4 kcal/mol further towards progesterone.&#x20;

The mutations alter how well the pocket holds a steroid, not which steroid it holds. Testosterone and progesterone differ only at C17, and if a substitution cannot distinguish that one group, it will always move both ligands together.

Two substitutions were examined in detail: V309T, which replaces a valine methyl group with the hydroxyl of threonine, and L311S, which trims a leucine side chain down to serine. Both were intended to introduce polarity near the steroid and make the pocket less comfortable for progesterone’s acetyl group. Both sit above the plane of the progesterone, over the B and C rings, while the C17 substituent extends past them into the open end of the pocket (<Ref target="fig-tonmw" /> and <Ref target="fig-bifbg" />).

Since neither mutated side chain reaches the C17 group, there is no direct contact. So, an acetyl group and a hydroxyl look the same to the enzyme, which matches the docking scores: every substitution at these positions moved both substrates by the same amount, leaving the selectivity gap at +0.5 kcal/mol.

AKR1D1 was therefore not carried forward. Engineering specificity here would need side chains long enough to reach C17 from these positions, or a different set of positions closer to it.

#### For ci2350:

Seventeen stable ci2350 variants were docked with both steroids. Selectivity is again ΔG(testosterone) − ΔG(progesterone), so a negative value means testosterone is bound more tightly. The wild type sits at −0.1 kcal/mol, a marginal preference that is within scoring noise.

**Combo 8 (R215A + V263I)** is the only variant that moved the preference by more than the **±0.5 kcal/mol** noise, shifting it **0.8 kcal/mol** from the wild type, and the combination is structurally well tolerated (**ΔΔG = +1.42 REU**). It is also the only one that does so without giving up affinity ([Table 2](tab-nn49s)): **progesterone at −8.5** and **testosterone at −9.4** are close to the wild-type values, so the gap comes from holding testosterone more tightly rather than from binding everything more weakly.

**R215** makes a **direct contact** with the **C17 acetyl tail** of progesterone, so replacing it with alanine removes a stabilising interaction that only progesterone uses. **V263I** works on the same region from the other side: isoleucine is bulkier than valine and appears to **crowd the acetyl tail**, while testosterone, which carries only a compact 17β-hydroxyl at that position, fits the branched side chain more comfortably and binds slightly better.

The two mutations behave very differently on their own. V263I shifts the preference by only −0.2 selectivity and weakens progesterone binding to −7.4 kcal/mol, so the gain in Combo 8 has to come from removing the arginine at 215 [(Table 2)](tab-nn49s).

**Combo 13 (R215A + V263I + Q100T)** adds Q100T to the Combo 8 backbone. **Q100** contacts the **17β-hydroxyl** of testosterone, and threonine offers the same hydrogen-bonding capacity from a shorter side chain, which may suit the compact hydroxyl better. In combination the variant reaches **ΔΔG = −7.19 REU**, the most stable candidate in the set, which helps in obtaining enough soluble protein to characterise. The cost is selectivity: the preference falls to −0.5 kcal/mol, within the noise floor. Combo 13 is therefore the safer choice if stability proves limiting, rather than the better one on specificity.

V263K also reaches −0.5 kcal/mol selectivity, but by a different route. The long lysine side chain is poorly suited to the hydrophobic end of the pocket and weakens binding of both steroids substantially, progesterone from −9.1 to −6.9 and testosterone from −9.2 to −7.4, with progesterone penalized more. The preference comes from making the pocket worse for everything rather than from distinguishing the two substituents. As a single substitution, it is simple to make and characterize, which keeps it on the list, but the affinity loss limits its usefulness.

Position 263 is clearly the main lever, it appears in eight of the nine top-ranked variants, but on its own it never produces a meaningful preference. Only when paired with R215A does it give a shift large enough to act on, so **Combo 8** was carried forward into the activity model [(Modeling)](https://wiki.igemlund.org/modeling#mathematical-modelling-of-enzyme-production-and-activity).&#x20;
