## **Description**

***We began with a simple question: could synthetic biology offer a new way of approaching PMOS?***

From there, we had to decide where our contribution could be most meaningful. PMOS is a complex and highly heterogeneous condition, with symptoms and priorities that vary between individuals. ~~OPPURE and no single intervention can address every aspect of it.~~ We therefore looked for a feature that was both biologically relevant and important to the people affected. Review of the literature ~~and patient stories~~ led us to hyperandrogenism. Androgen excess contributes directly to dermatological features but interferes also at the ovarian and more broadely at the systemic level, creating a reinforcing cycle between endocrine imbalance and metabolic impairment, hyperandrogenism therefore became the focus of our project and the starting point for our engineering.

The project begins with the people who spent years trying to have the complexity of their condition recognised. Their experiences remind us that developing a new solution is not only a question of whether it can work biologically. It must also respond to a real need and be described, tested and communicated with greater precision than the problem it is intended to address.
