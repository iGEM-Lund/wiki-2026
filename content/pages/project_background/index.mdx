## **Description**

We began with a simple question: could synthetic biology offer a new way of approaching PMOS?

From there, we had to decide where such an approach could be most meaningful. PMOS is a complex and highly heterogeneous condition, and no single intervention can address every aspect of it. We therefore looked for a clinical feature that was both common and consistently described ~~as a one of the major burdens and something that was fueling the PMOS loop by people living with the syndrome.~~ Again and again, the evidence pointed towards hyperandrogenism. Androgen excess is one of the central features of PMOS and can manifest as hirsutism, acne and androgen-related hair loss, with consequences that extend far beyond their physical presentation. For many people, these symptoms affect self-image, emotional wellbeing and everyday quality of life REFERENCES. Hyperandrogenism therefore became the focus of our project and the starting point for our engineering.

But the project does not begin with the engineered organism. It begins with the people who spent years trying to have the complexity of their condition recognised. Their experiences remind us that developing a new solution is not only a question of whether it can work biologically. It must also respond to a real need and be described, tested and communicated with greater precision than the problem it is intended to address.
