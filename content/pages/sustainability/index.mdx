She emphasized that PMOS is highly complex and that treatment needs to differ depending on symptoms, age, and life stage. Based on her feedback, we decided to define our target patient group and treatment goal more clearly, while paying greater attention to the safety and limitations of androgen reduction, particularly for women who may become pregnant.

  Summary of interview:

  We contacted Suman Rice because of her expertise in reproductive physiology and women’s reproductive health. We wanted her perspective on whether reducing androgen activity could be a reasonable therapeutic approach for PMOS and what physiological factors we should consider.

  We discussed androgen production and regulation in PMOS, the roles of testosterone and aromatase, and limitations of current treatments such as antiandrogen pills and metformin. We also discussed our gut-based approach, suitable target populations, and potential safety concerns.

  She emphasized that PMOS involves multiple interconnected hormonal and metabolic pathways, so targeting testosterone alone may not address the whole condition. She also advised us to clearly define which patient group and symptoms we aim to target, and highlighted the importance of considering systemic effects and pregnancy safety when developing an androgen-reducing treatment. 

  Integration:

  **Safety**: We added pregnancy and fetal safety as an important consideration, since reducing androgen activity could pose risks during pregnancy. 

  **Gut mechanism**: We reconsidered how gut localized treatment could affect systemic androgen levels.
