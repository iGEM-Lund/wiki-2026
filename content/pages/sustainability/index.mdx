<Quote quote="Sustainable development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." quotee="UN's Brundtland Commission, report &#x22;Our Common Future&#x22;, 1987" />

When asked what is sustainability, the first associations that come to people's mind are environmental: protect the environment, reduce pollution and be less wasteful. However, this represents only **one of three pillars**, as defined by Brundtland report, with economic viability and social equity given equal importance. A truly sustainable solution must be economically feasible to implement and scale, while also promoting fair access to resources and opportunities. All three pillars must work together, since addressing one while ignoring the others creates new problems. Thus, this sustainable development page should be read along with [Education](https://wiki.igemlund.org/education) and [Human Practices](wiki.igemlund.org/human-practices) pages to see how environmental, economic, and social considerations were integrated throughout our project.

To make sustainable development concrete, the United Nations adopted the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in 2015 <Citation reference="united-nations-n-d" />. This framework provides 17 specific goals that address major global challenges, from poverty and hunger, to clean energy and climate action. The SDGs serve as a roadmap for coordinated international progress toward a more sustainable world by 2030.

However, the Sustainable Development Goals Report of 2026 shows that only over a third of the SDGs are on track to being achieved by the set deadline (WILL ADD CONNECTION, JUST found that report, quite fascinating - e.g.: This is why we do not only list what our project contributes, but also where it and our team may do harm.).

For iGEM Lund, sustainability was a priority from the start. We represent Lund University, which ranks first globally in the QS Sustainability Rankings <Citation reference="qs-university-rankings-2026" />, and we felt a responsibility to align our project with these principles. We also decided to be specific and focused instead of wide and shallow, so we selected five goals: SDG 3, 4, 5, 12 and 17.

We built sustainability into every part of our project. This page brings it together in two steps: first, what AnTigone and our team contribute to SDG 3, 4, 5, 12 and 17 (LINK); then, where they may work against these goals (LINK).

## Where We May Do Harm

The goals above describe what our project and team aim to contribute. An honest SDG evaluation also asks where we might work against a goal, because a project can help one goal and hurt another. Here we name where our team and our product may work against the goals, why we accept it, and what we do about it. The first group comes from **how we work**, the second from **what we are building**.

### How We Work

These impacts come from how we, as a student team, run our project: where we teach, how we work in the lab, and how we travel.

<Card>
  <CardTitle image="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/sustainability/e-web-goal-04.avif" subtitle="Inflicting SDG 4: “Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all.”">
    Education Reach
  </CardTitle>

  SDG 4 asks for inclusive and equitable quality education.&#x20;

  Our reach has stayed narrow in geography, language and audience. Our events took place in Lund (BioCamp, two gymnasium workshops, PMOS Awareness Month, the K-Guild Study Night and the sustainability lecture), in Strängnäs (Rays for Excellence) and in Malmö (library). Most materials are English-only, the podcast is on Spotify, which is not accessible everywhere, and most participants were students already in science programmes. We did not measure lasting learning.

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    This is how we are dealing with it:&#x20;

    * English is our primary and mostly only language. That is a limitation, but simultaneously also the most inclusive way. We introduced more languages for the material that mattered most, children’s books, but besides that we did not further focus on having multiple translations, but we would call that defendable. 
    * Our children's book exists in six languages, Chinese, Spanish, German, Swedish, English, and Italian, genuinely global reach for that specific material, not just an English product.
    * Our podcast, The Sequence, is built specifically to pull in perspectives from outside our own bubble: we've talked with other iGEM teams from e.g. Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Taiwan, and India about their projects, struggles, and journeys. Spotify itself has accessibility limits, but the content is deliberately not Sweden- or Europe-only.
    * The concentration around Lund is partly a deliberate choice, not only a gap: as a small student team, we can have a real, measurable effect on the community immediately around us in a way we couldn't replicate by spreading thin across a global audience we don't have the capacity to actually serve. We think it's more honest to say we chose local depth over global breadth than to claim we achieved both.
  </Expandable>
</Card>

<Card>
  <CardTitle image="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/sustainability/e-web-goal-05.avif" subtitle="Inflicting SDG 5.6: “Ensure universal access to sexual and reproductive health and reproductive rights […]”">
    **Inclusive Framing (NOT FINISHED)**
  </CardTitle>

  SDG 5.6 protects universal access to reproductive health and rights, which, under the Beijing Platform for Action it invokes, includes access to relevant information and freedom from **discrimination**. Our flyers, presentations, and educational materials have so far defaulted to "women" throughout. PMOS is tied to ovarian function, not gender identity, trans men and non-binary AFAB people can have it too, and remain underrepresented in related research. When materials only ever say "women's health," people outside that framing may not recognize the information applies to them at all, a real, if indirect, barrier to the access this target is meant to protect.

  How we're dealing with it: revising our wiki and patient-facing materials to explicitly name AFAB and trans people alongside "women," with a short note on why. This was implemented in the patient’s survey and the wiki. (LINK)
</Card>
