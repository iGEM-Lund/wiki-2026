## Introduction

When asked what is **sustainability**, the first associations that come to people's mind are environmental: protect the environment, reduce pollution and be less wasteful. However, this represents only **one of three pillars**, as defined by Brundtland report, with economic viability and social equity given equal importance. A truly sustainable solution must be economically feasible to implement and scale, while also promoting fair access to resources and opportunities. All three pillars must work together, since addressing one while ignoring the others creates new problems. Thus, this sustainable development page should be read along with [Education](/education) and [Human Practices](/human-practices) pages to see how environmental, economic, and social considerations were integrated throughout our project.

<Quote quote="Sustainable development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." quotee="UN's Brundtland Commission, report &#x22;Our Common Future&#x22;, 1987" />

&#x20;To make sustainable development concrete, the United Nations (UN) adopted the **17 Sustainable Development Goals** (SDGs) in 2015 (<Citation reference="united-nations-n-d" />). This framework provides 17 specific goals that address major global challenges, from poverty and hunger, to clean energy and climate action. The SDGs serve as a roadmap for coordinated international progress toward a more sustainable world by **2030**.

However, the Sustainable Development Goals Report of 2026 shows that **only over a third of the SDGs are on track** to being achieved by the set deadline. Thus we wanted to contribute to the SDGs any way we could, to help bring them closer to reality. This is why we do not only list what our project contributes, but also where it and our team may do harm.

For **iGEM Lund**, sustainability was a priority from the start. We represent Lund University, which ranks first globally in the **QS Sustainability Rankings** <Citation reference="qs-university-rankings-2026" />, and we felt a responsibility to align our project with these principles. We also decided to be specific and focused instead of wide and shallow, so we selected five goals: **SDG 3, 4, 5, 12 and 17**.

We built sustainability into every part of our project. This page brings it together in two steps: first, what AnTigone and our team contribute to **SDG 3, 4, 5, 12 and 17** (LINK); then, where they may work against these goals (LINK).

    ### SDG 3: Good health and well-being
  </CardTitle>

  This goal focuses on ensuring **good health and promoting well-being for everyone**, at all ages. Of the many targets under this goal, **targets** **3.4, 3.7** and **3.8** ties with the project, where our potential product and project activities aim to  promoting mental health, to ensuring universal access to reproductive services, and to providing better access to essential health-care services and access to safe, effective, quality and affordable essential medicines for all[ \[SDG 3\]](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal3#targets_and_indicators). 

  **Target 3.4** addresses the reduction of premature mortality from non-communicable diseases and the promotion of mental health and well-being. This is of relevance to us as the **mental health burden** in PMOS is considerable. Women with the condition have around four times the odds of depressive symptoms and more than five times the odds of anxiety symptoms, when compared to controls (<Citation reference="cooney-et-al-2017" />). The international guidelines therefore recommend that every patient is screened for both conditions (<Citation reference="teede-et-al-2023" />). 

  **Target 3.7** addresses universal access to sexual and reproductive health-care services. PMOS is the most common cause of <Tooltip text="Release of egg from the ovaries does not occur, often due to hormonal imbalances
  https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21698-anovulation ">**anovulatory infertility**</Tooltip> and is also a leading reason for women seeking fertility care (<Citation reference="teede-et-al-2023" />). 

  **Target 3.8** addresses universal health coverage and access to safe, effective, quality and affordable essential medicines.

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    **Why are we addressing it?**

    **Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome** (PMOS), is the most common <Tooltip text="The endocrine system is responsible for the release and control of hormones in the body.
    https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21201-endocrine-system">**endocrine**</Tooltip>**&#x20;and reproductive condition** affecting women, yet it remains largely **under-diagnosed** or **misdiagnosed**, with a prevalence of around 10  to 13% and with up to 70% of the affected women worldwide remaining undiagnosed (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2025" />). 

    There are several reasons behind this and they tend to reinforce one another. PMOS is a **complex condition** affecting multiple systems and its presentation varies considerably from one woman to the next. There is therefore no single symptom that reliably identifies it. It also sits within the broader <Tooltip text="Systematic difference in medical research, healthcare provision, and health outcomes between men and women.
    https://patient.info/features/womens-health/breaking-down-the-gender-health-gap">**gender health gap**</Tooltip>, where women continue to be under-represented in clinical research. An analysis of 1,442 high impact trials, enrolling over 4.7 million participants, found that only 44% of the participants were female and that fewer than a quarter of the studies achieved sex matching >80%, even after the policy reforms, such as the NIH Revitalization Act of 1993 and the 2016 Sex as a Biological Variable policy, which were meant to correct this (<Citation reference="barlek-et-al-2022" />). 

    Women also report more often that their symptoms were dismissed or were attributed to something else, before a diagnosis was finally reached. In PMOS specifically, it has been documented that **a third of the women waited** **more than two years** and consulted three or more health professionals before being diagnosed (<Citation reference="gibson-helm-et-al-2017" />). The former name of the condition added to this situation and was not the single cause of it, as the condition was named before as **Poly Cystic Ovarian Syndrome** (PCOS), before being renamed to **Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome** (PMOS) early in 2026 (<Citation reference="teede-et-al-2026" />), as the name PCOS presented the wrong impression that the defining feature was the presence of ovarian cysts, and this impression went on to shape both the research priorities and the clinical expectations around the condition. 

    However the condition's real defining features are hormonal imbalance (**hyperandrogenism**), irregular <Tooltip text="maturation of an ovarian follicle https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532300/">follicular development</Tooltip> and metabolic disruptions (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2026" />), with hyperandrogenism being the most prominent feature for diagnosis (<Citation reference="forslund-et-al-2026" />). This condition also increases the chances of anovulation and infertility and also raises the odds of adverse outcomes during pregnancy, such as <Tooltip text="High blood sugar that develops during pregnancy.
    https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gestational-diabetes/">gestational diabetes</Tooltip>, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, pre-term birth and higher rates of caesarean delivery (<Citation reference="bahri-khomami-et-al-2024" />). 

    The current existing treatments include the use of **hormonal pills, lifestyle changes and the use of therapeutics**, either as a standalone plan or in combination (<Citation reference="panchpuri-et-al-2026" />). These treatments manage the symptoms and not the androgen excess which drives them. They also come with their own trade-offs. They act on the whole body and not on one site, they are not compatible with trying to conceive, and they are intended to be taken over the long term or for life (<Citation reference="teede-et-al-2023" />). 

    Our project proposes a concept for a potential **hormone-free** option for people with PMOS-related hyperandrogenism. It is not a cure and would not treat the root cause of PMOS. It is designed to **lower androgen activity** in the gut, where androgens are reactivated and recirculated, and so to reduce hyperandrogenism symptoms in people for whom current treatments don’t work or aren’t tolerated. 

    **How this relates to the targets:**

    * **Target 3.4 (mental health and well-being):** Hyperandrogenism symptoms such as acne, excess hair growth, and hair loss affect **self-esteem** and mood in many patients (see the burden of depression and anxiety above). A treatment that reduces these symptoms could ease part of this burden, but we have not shown that it does.
    * **Target 3.7 (information and education on reproductive health):** Through our PMOS Awareness Month events and the consistent use of the new PMOS name, we worked to close the knowledge gap around the condition for students and the public (see [SDG 5](/sustainability#sdg-5-gender-equality)).
    * **Target 3.8 (access to affordable essential medicines):** For AnTigone, this target cuts both ways. Some design choices could improve access: an **oral capsule** is easy to distribute and take which matters for a condition managed over many years, and engineered probiotics can be designed to act locally in the gut, limiting exposure elsewhere in the body (<Citation reference="liu-et-al-2026" />). At the same time, other features of the product work against affordability and access, discussed in the [infliction](/sustainability#product-based-inflictions) part of this page.

    ### SDG 4: Good Health and Well-Being
  </CardTitle>

  This goal focuses on ensuring that **quality education** and lifelong learning opportunities are available to everyone. Because education is one of our three specialisations, we address **SDG 4** as a whole, and two of its targets. **Target 4.4** aims to substantially increase the number of youth and adults who have relevant skills, including technical and vocational skills. **Target** **4.7** aims to ensure that all learners acquire the knowledge and skills needed to promote sustainable development, including through education for sustainable development and sustainable lifestyles, human rights and gender equality [\[SDG 4\]](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal4#targets_and_indicators)**.**

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    **Why are we addressing it?**

    Our education work stands on three pillars: **PMOS awareness, synthetic biology literacy** and **sustainability**. Each answers a gap in what people know. Synthetic biology literacy is the pillar covered under this goal. PMOS awareness is covered under [**SDG 5**](/sustainability#:~:text=SDG%205:%20Gender%20Equality) and sustainability under [**SDG 12**](/sustainability#sdg-12-responsible-consumption-and-production).

    **Synthetic biology**, the discipline our solution is built on, is poorly understood outside the field. Nationally representative survey work found that the public often does not feel informed about it (<Citation reference="akin-et-al-2017" />). This matters for us: if we propose an engineered organism as a treatment, people need a way to understand what that means. School curricula rarely give students the chance to work with these tools. After our [bioinformatics workshops](/education#:~:text=Bioinformatics%20Workshop), students told us they found it interesting to see biotechnology applied in real life, because they were used to facts in textbooks.

    We therefore focused our work here on teaching real, transferable skills (**target** **4.4**), not just introducing the field, and we chose **young students** as our main audience, since they are choosing their further education. **Target** **4.7**, education for sustainable development and gender equality, is carried by the other two pillars: PMOS awareness under [**SDG 5**](/sustainability#sdg-5-gender-equality) and sustainability under [**SDG 12**](/sustainability#sdg-12-responsible-consumption-and-production).

    **How are we implementing this?**

    **Target** **4.4**: relevant technical skills. The participants did the work themselves.

    1. Dry Lab Introduction (9 April) (LINK): Seven team members were trained in a full workflow for characterising candidate enzymes: motif analysis, AlphaFold3 structure prediction, structural alignment, docking and active-site conservation.
    2. [BioCamp](/education#:~:text=Student%20workshop%20at%20Ung%20Vetenskapssport) (25 April, with Ung Vetenskapssport): About 30 high school students from across Sweden did a codon-amino acid exercise, built a plasmid as a craft activity and developed a solution to a real-world problem.
    3. [Rays for Excellence](/education#:~:text=Workshop%20at%20summer%20school%20Rays%20for%20Excellence) (1 to 2 July, Strängnäs): Rays for Excellence is an academic summer programme for Swedish high school students interested in science, technology, engineering and mathematics. We contributed one **two-day workshop** to it, for about 25 students. On day one we gave a lecture on recombinant protein expression and gene regulation, introduced PMOS and our project, and let the students solve a mini iGEM challenge in teams. On day two they put the theory into practice in a **hands-on lab**, performing a bacterial transformation to express GFP and interpreting their results. Feedback was very positive, and students were especially engaged in the lab.
    4. [Bioinformatics workshops at two gymnasiums](/education#:~:text=Bioinformatics%20Workshop) (2 and 5 October): At Lars Erik Larsson Gymnasiet (natural science programme, two classes, 75 minutes each) and Hedda Andersson Gymnasiet (biotechnology advanced course, one class, 70 minutes), students worked with two unknown protein sequences. They identified them with **BLAST** and **PDB** or **UniProt** and compared them in **Clustal Omega**, then connected the results to our project, including identifying one sequence as the androgen receptor. 

    Reaching beyond the classroom (goal level). These activities addressed learners outside school.

    1. [Colouring book *The Tiny Helpers Inside You*:](/education#the-tiny-helpers-inside-you-coloring-book--library-visit) It introduces children aged 3 to 5 to the idea that cells send and receive signals. 18 copies (9 English, 9 Swedish) were placed at Malmö City Library (LINK), and all had been taken by the next day’s noon.
    2. [Podcast *The Sequence*:](https://open.spotify.com/show/1anr7zYZ3pKdntKfGcVEvz?si=j3_TYq5HQr-aZCCynVjoTQ\&utm_source=copy-link) 23 episodes so far, mixing interviews with iGEM teams around the world and fun facts from team members. 
    3. KULA career fair (12 February): (LINK) We spoke with about 30 chemistry and biotechnology students about iGEM and synthetic biology.

    **Note**: Most events reached students already in science or engineering programmes, mostly in Lund or Sweden in general. We measured attendance and feedback, not lasting learning, and we do not claim effects on employment. See more about the limitations later this page under “[inflictions](/sustainability#sdg-inflictions)”.

    ### SDG &#x35;**: Gender Equality**
  </CardTitle>

  **Goal 5** broadly addresses gender inequality and **target 5.6** specifically focuses on achieving universal access to sexual and reproductive health and reproductive rights, giving women the autonomy to make their own informed decisions regarding their reproductive health care [\[**SDG 5**\]](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5#targets_and_indicators). This fits our work partly: PMOS is an endocrine and reproductive condition, and our awareness work supports informed decisions and earlier diagnosis. We do not claim to change access to care itself.

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    **Why are we addressing it?**

    PMOS affects roughly **10 to 13% of women** of reproductive age, and up to **70%** remain undiagnosed (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2026" />). It has been documented that research on this condition was focused mostly on symptoms such as ovarian cysts, which has contributed to missed and late diagnosis and significant distress (<Citation reference="gibson-helm-et-al-2017" />). The old name PCOS gave the wrong impression that the defining feature was the presence of ovarian cysts. It was replaced by PMOS in 2026 (Teede H et al., 2026), but the new name is still unfamiliar to most people and many clinicians. This sits within the wider gender health gap, where women remain under-represented in clinical research (Barlek M et al., 2022).

    Our product aims to **lower androgen activity**, since hyperandrogenism is a defining feature of PMOS. It is still hypothetical, and it would not address every aspect of the condition. We therefore also work on the information side: this is our [PMOS awareness pillar](/education#:~:text=Our%20Approach%20to%20Education), and the education part of what **SDG 3.7** asks for (see [**SDG 3**](/sustainability#:~:text=SDG%203:%20Good%20health%20and%20well%2Dbeing)).

    Lifestyle changes, including healthy eating and physical activity, are recommended for all women with PMOS as a core part of management (Teede H et al., 2023). We used this when choosing formats: movement and food were part of most of our events.

    **How are we implementing this?**

    The activities we ran under this goal were aimed at the people whose decisions about their own reproductive health are the most affected by poor information. 

    1. **Using PMOS instead of PCOS** (since May 2026) (LINK): After the name change we used PMOS on our wiki, slides, socials and flyers, also next to “PCOS” so that it stayed recognisable. 
    2. **PMOS Awareness Month (September)**. We ran mostly informal, low-barrier events, each with a PMOS introduction and from the second event onward a printed PMOS fact sheet, also introducing the name change.
       1. A 2.5 km [awareness run](/education#:~:text=PMOS%20Awareness%20Month-,PMOS%20Awareness%20Run,-Date%20&%20Location:%201) in Lund (1 September, 11 participants).
       2. [Awareness Volleyball](/education#:~:text=PMOS%20Awareness%20Volleyball%20Event) (12 September, about 25 participants). 
       3. [Awareness Study Night](/education#:~:text=Read%20more-,K%2DGuild%20Study%20Night,-Date%20&%20Location:%2017) with the K-Guild (17 September, about 70 students) with a PMOS-friendly food served. After the PMOS introduction talk, a student with PMOS even thanked us for raising awareness.
       4. [Awareness Yoga](/education#:~:text=PMOS%20Awareness%20Yoga%20Session) with Smålands Nation (27 September, 23 participants). 
       5. [Workshop with Future by Lund](/education#:~:text=more-,PMOS%20Awareness%20Workshop,-Date) (9 September). 23 students from several fields worked in groups on anonymised patient files with lab results, hormone profiles and scans, then presented their diagnoses. We also discussed the gender health gap. All 13 respondents rated the event good or excellent, and 46% said they learned a lot. 

    [**Clinician survey**](/human-practices#:~:text=Clinician%20Survey,-Clinician%20perspectives%20on)**:** We asked clinicians about their experience with PMOS care and the name change.

    **Note**: Activity-based events produced less PMOS learning than the awareness workshop, but they reached people who would not attend a lecture. Also, our materials speak mostly about “women”, which leaves out some people with PMOS (see Inclusive Framing under inflictions LINK). Reach was local and mostly students.

    ### SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production
  </CardTitle>

  **Goal 12** focuses on producing and consuming within **environmental limits**. It covers the efficient use of resources, the responsible management of chemicals and waste across their whole life cycle and also the provision of the information people need in order to make sustainable choices. 

  The indicators for **target** **12.4** focuses on the track compliance of countries in accordance with international agreements and hazardous wastes generated per capita and proportion properly treated. **Target** **12.5** focuses on reducing the wastes generated by systematically preventing, reducing, recycling and reusing whenever and wherever possible. **Target** **12.8** focuses on ensuring that people everywhere have the relevant information and awareness for sustainable development and lifestyles grounded in harmony with nature[ \[**SDG 12**\]](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal12#targets_and_indicators)

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    **Why are we addressing it?**

    The synthetic hormones that are currently used in the treatment of hyperandrogenism belong to a class of endocrine-disrupting chemicals. **Ethinylestradiol** is the oestrogen component of the combined oral contraceptives routinely prescribed in PMOS. It is among the compounds which conventional wastewater treatment plants are not able to remove to the degree required. It has also been measured in sewage effluent at concentrations of **65 to 187 ng per litre**, against a predicted no-effect concentration of only **0.035 ng per litre** (<Citation reference="klaica-et-al-2022" />). 

    Conventional wastewater treatment does not completely remove them and these chemicals end up persisting in aquatic bodies, causing **considerable harm to aquatic life and humans** (<Citation reference="kidd-et-al-2007" />). As our product is designed to be a probiotic, it can be assumed that the production and manufacturing of the product will involve considerably less use of chemicals, lesser use of solvents, lower step reactions, lower purification steps and also less damaging to the environment than traditional pharmaceutical drugs (<Citation reference="wilkinson-et-al-2022" />). 

    **Note**: If the product worked and would replace some hormonal treatment, it could reduce environmental effects, but that is a **hypothesis**. We have not assessed the footprint of its potential manufacturing or cold chain, and as a live genetically modified organism it brings **biocontainment questions** that hormones do not (see [Environmental & Biocontainment](/sustainability#:~:text=Environmental%20&%20Biocontainment%20Footprint) under Inflictions). We therefore treat sustainability as something to design and practise, starting in our own lab, and not as a claim.

    **How are we implementing this?**

    1. [**Biosafety**](/biosafety#:~:text=Genetic%20and%20ecological%20safety:)**&#x20;by design** (**target** **12.4**). Our biosafety committee worked in three layers: in-host control, environmental biocontainment and regulation.
       1. [In-host control](/biosafety#in-host-control): A **hypoxia-responsive gut gate** so that the enzyme is only expressed in the oxygen-poor gut, plus a **proposed testosterone-sensing feedback loop** combined with it as an AND gate. We chose a transient design that relies on redosing, because wild-type *E. coli Nissle 1917* persists in the gut for weeks. The effect on resident gut bacteria remains an open question.
       2. [Environmental biocontainment](/biosafety#environmental-biocontainment): **ΔdapA (DAP) auxotrophy** as the chosen primary strategy and a **CRISPR kill switch** driven by oxygen. Both are still design-stage. We also considered genetic stability, since mutation can restore growth.
       3. [Horizontal gene transfer](/biosafety#:~:text=horizontal%20gene%20transfer%20\(HGT\)) and [regulation](/biosafety#gmo-regulations): **Chromosomal integration** is planned to limit gene transfer, and we reviewed the EU rules on contained use, release and medicinal use, the EMA environmental risk assessment guideline and the <Tooltip text="The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety to the Convention on Biological Diversity is an international agreement which aims to ensure the safe handling, transport and use of living modified organisms. https://bch.cbd.int/protocol">Cartagena Protocol</Tooltip>.
    2. **Chemicals in our own lab (target 12.4): (LINNK?)** We used **green chemicals** where possible and avoided CMR reagents, with testosterone the only one that was necessary. We also avoided other toxic chemicals such as ethidium bromide, but some had to be used, such as imidazole and NiCl. Changing kanamycin to ampicillin or spectinomycin made waste disposal safer. 
    3. [**Learning first, then teaching**](/education#:~:text=Sustainability%20in%20the%20Lab:%20Internal%20Workshop)**&#x20;(target 12.8):** On 1 June the wet lab team (9 of 14 members) worked though the **My Green Lab Ambassador Certificate** (add link) (energy, plastic, water, green chemistry). A follow-up meeting with [Johan Teljer](/human-practices#:~:text=Johan%20Tejler,-Work%20Environment%20Coordinator) showed that <Tooltip text="Building, where our lab was located.">Kemicentrum</Tooltip> already applies measures such as lower water pressure. On 28 August we gave a **sustainability lecture** on lab practice to 17 LTH students, with a live Mentimeter quiz and group discussion, and with content reviewed by [Christina Wanneberg](/human-practices#:~:text=Christina%20Wennerberg). We wanted it to be mandatory for first-year students but could not achieve that in time, so it was optional.

    ### SDG 17: Partnerships for the Goals, Strengthen the means of implementation and revitalize the global partnership for sustainable development
  </CardTitle>

  **Goal 17** recognises that none of the other goals can be reached by any single actor working on its own. It is concerned with the finance, the technology, the capacity building, the trade and the partnership arrangements which make the rest of the agenda possible. The **target** **17.17** focuses on promoting effective public, public-private and civil society partnerships, building on the experience and resourcing strategies of partnerships[ \[**SDG 17**\]](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal17#targets_and_indicators).

  <Expandable defaultOpen={false}>
    ---

    **Why are we addressing it?**

    PMOS affects roughly 10 to 13% of women of reproductive age, and up to 70% remain undiagnosed (WHO, 2025). Its former name contributed to delayed diagnosis and fragmented care (<Citation reference="teede-et-al-2026" />). No single student team can change this situation, and a project built around an engineered organism also needs input from people who see the problem from the clinical, academic and commercial side, not only from the bench. We therefore treated **partnerships** as part of the science and not as something arranged around it: with other iGEM teams, academia, industry, clinicians and civil society organisations such as student guilds, a library and a summer school. Our longer-term aim is a **research community** that recognises PMOS for what it is, a complex endocrine and metabolic condition and not a disorder of ovarian cysts. We cannot claim this change, but better-informed people ask better questions.

    **How are we implementing this?**

    1. **Academia and research** - We consulted researchers working on PMOS and androgen biology, including [Elisabet Stener-Victorin](/human-practices#:~:text=Elisabet%20Stener%2DVictorin), [Anna Benrick](/human-practices#:~:text=Anna%20Benrick,-Professor%20in%20Endocrinology) and [Trevor Penning](/human-practices#:~:text=Trevor%20M.%20Penning,-Professor%20in%20Pharmacology).
    2. **Clinicians** - [Angelica Lindén Hirschberg](/human-practices#:~:text=Angelica%20Lindén%20Hirschberg), [Lina Zabulienė](/human-practices#:~:text=Lina%20Zabulienė,-Endocrinologist%20with%20expertise), [Katia Palombino](/human-practices#:~:text=Katia%20Palombino,-Gynecologist%20and%20endocrinologist) and [Eleni Dubé-Zinatelli](/human-practices#:~:text=Eleni%20Dubé%20Zinatelli,-PhD) shared their clinical perspective, and we invited clinicians in several countries (Sweden, Denmark, Germany, Italy, Portugal and Lithuania, among others) to our clinician survey.
    3. **Lund University and other institutions** - We met [Francesco Bez](/human-practices#:~:text=Francesco%20Bez,-Innovation%20Manager%20Medicon) (Medicon Village) and [Nicolas Arriagada](/human-practices#:~:text=Nicolas%20Arriagada,-Innovation%20Developer%20LU) (LU Innovation), [Johan Teljer](/human-practices#:~:text=Johan%20Tejler,-Work%20Environment%20Coordinator) on sustainability at Kemicentrum, and [Lars Mattiasson](/human-practices#:~:text=Lars%20Mattiasson,-Business%20developer%20and) at Future by Lund, with whom we also ran the PMOS workshop. We also spoke with [SciLifeLab](/human-practices#:~:text=Simon%20Ekström,-Head%20of%20unit).
    4. **Experts for safety, ethics and education** - [Dr. Clement T.Y. ## SDG Inflictions

We took this approach from our meeting with **Magdalena Nour** (Vati of Sweden), who advised us not to avoid every negative impact but to be conscious about them, explain why they happen and be transparent.&#x20;

<Quote quote="Transparency is the currency. Transparency is what you should focus on." quotee="Magdalena Nour, CEO of Vati of Sweden, in conversation with iGEM Lund, 8 September 2026" figure="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/human-practices/magdalena-nour.avif" />

We therefore name where our team and our product may work against the goals, why we accept it, and what we do about it. The first group comes from how we as a team work, the second from a hypothetical product.

### Team Based Inflictions

These impacts come from how we, as a student team, run our project: where we teach, how we work in the lab, and how we travel.

<Card>
  <CardTitle image="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/sustainability/e-web-goal-04.avif" subtitle="Inflicting SDG 4: “Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all.”">
    Education Reach
  </CardTitle>

  **SDG 4** asks for inclusive and equitable quality education.&#x20;

  Our reach has stayed narrow in geography, language and audience. Our events took place in **Lund** (BioCamp, two gymnasium workshops, PMOS Awareness Month, the K-Guild Study Night and the sustainability lecture), in **Strängnäs** (Rays for Excellence) and in **Malmö** (library). Most materials are English-only, the podcast is on Spotify, which is not accessible everywhere, and most participants were students already in science programmes. We did not measure lasting learning.

  ---

  **This is how we are dealing with it:**&#x20;

  * English is our primary and mostly only language. That is a limitation, but simultaneously also the most inclusive way. We introduced more languages for the material that mattered most, children’s books, but besides that we did not further focus on having multiple translations, but we would call that defendable. 
  * Our **children's book** exists in six languages, Chinese, Spanish, German, Swedish, English, and Italian, genuinely global reach for that specific material, not just an English product.
  * Our podcast, **The Sequence**, is built specifically to pull in perspectives from outside our own bubble: we've talked with other iGEM teams from e.g. Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Taiwan, and India about their projects, struggles, and journeys. Spotify itself has accessibility limits, but the content is deliberately not Sweden- or Europe-only.
  * The concentration around Lund is partly a deliberate choice, not only a gap: as a small student team, we can have a real, measurable effect on the community immediately around us in a way we couldn't replicate by spreading thin across a global audience we don't have the capacity to actually serve. We think it's more honest to say we chose local depth over global breadth than to claim we achieved both.

  **Why we accept it:** spreading the same effort across many places would have meant less quality everywhere.

    **Inclusive Framing (NOT FINISHED)**
  </CardTitle>

  **SDG 5.6** protects universal access to reproductive health and rights, which, under the <Tooltip text="Global agenda for the achievement of gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls. https://www.undp.org/ukraine/publications/what-beijing-platform-action">Beijing Platform for Action</Tooltip> it invokes, includes access to relevant information and freedom from **discrimination (**<Citation reference="un-women-2014" />). Our flyers, presentations, and educational materials have so far defaulted to "women" throughout. PMOS is tied to ovarian function, not gender identity, trans men and non-binary <Tooltip text="assigned female at birth ">AFAB</Tooltip> people can have it too, and remain underrepresented in related research (<Citation reference="wugalter-et-al-2023" />). When materials only ever say "women's health," people outside that framing may not recognize the information applies to them at all, a real, if indirect, barrier to the access this target is meant to protect.

  ---

  **How we're dealing with it:** revising our wiki and patient-facing materials to explicitly name AFAB and trans people alongside "women," with a short note on why. This was implemented in the patient’s survey and the wiki. (LINK)

  **Why we accept it:** we only accept it partly. Most sources we rely on (for example WHO and the Lancet consensus) say “women”, and early on we adopted that wording without questioning it. Once we noticed (rather late) we added it where we could and where it mattered most, in the survey and on the wiki.&#x20;

    #### Wet Lab Safety
  </CardTitle>

  **SDG 8.8** calls for safe and secure working environments for all workers. Our wet lab work carries real risk: chemical handling, biological materials, live engineered organisms, testosterone (a controlled steroid hormone), and machinery like UV light and the autoclave.

  ---

  **How we're dealing with it:**

  * **Mandatory Lund University safety training** and quiz before anyone touches the lab: <Tooltip text="Personal Protetive Equipment">PPE</Tooltip>, risk assessment, how to handle, store, transport, and label chemicals, lab warning signs, waste disposal (<Citation reference="kemicentrum-n-d" />).
  * **Risk assessment** for every activity, safety data sheets accessible for every chemical used.
  * Testosterone specifically gets its own training, it's a controlled substance also used in doping.
  * Emergency procedures covered: eye washes, safety showers, fire extinguishers and blankets, emergency numbers, first aid for injuries.
  * Basic lab rule: never work alone in the lab.

  **Why we accept it:** the enzymes had to be tested in a wet lab, but the risks were minimized with the actions above.
</Card>

### Product Based Inflictions

These impacts concern the **hypothetical** product. They describe what could happen if it was ever developed.

<Card>
  <CardTitle image="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/sustainability/e-web-goal-03.avif" subtitle="Inflicting SDG 3.8: “Achieve universal health coverage […] and access to safe, effective, quality and affordable essential medicines and vaccines for all” and SDG 10 (goal level): “Reduce inequality within and among countries.”">
    Access & Affordability (NOT DONE)
  </CardTitle>

  **Target 3.8** asks for universal health coverage and access to safe, effective, quality and affordable essential medicines. **SDG 10** asks for reduced inequality within and among countries. AnTigone could **deepen inequality** if it were ever developed. A product that needs a diagnosis, a prescription, specialised manufacturing and possibly cold storage reaches first the people who already have the best access to care, and PMOS is under-diagnosed everywhere.

  ---

  **Access:** An estimated 10 to 13% of women of reproductive age have PMOS, and up to 70% do not know it (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2026" />). Diagnosis depends on health systems, which vary between countries. Globally, **4.6 billion** people lack access to essential health services, and women, people living in poverty and people in rural areas report greater difficulty accessing them (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2025" />). A product that needs a diagnosis and a prescription therefore reaches first the people who are already diagnosed, who are the people with the best access to care. As a live biotherapeutic, AnTigone would also need **manufacturing under strict quality control, storage and distribution conditions** that we have not tested (possibly cold chain), and approval under the medicinal and GMO rules of each country (see the biosafety section LINK).

  **Affordability**: Cost is a major barrier already; in three-quarters of countries with data, medicines account for at least 55% of people’s out-of-pocket health spending, and **2.1 billion** people face financial hardship to access care (<Citation reference="world-health-organization-2025" />). Live biotherapeutics have so far been priced as **specialty drugs**. One course of Vowst, an oral product for recurrent *C. difficile* infection, has an estimated retail price of **$19,500 USD** (<Citation reference="smith-and-roy-2024" />). This treats a different condition, but it shows what price range is possible. Our price estimate is \[LINK to financial assessment]. Whether insurance would pay depends on national decisions (in Sweden, [TLV](https://www.tlv.se/in-english.html)), and repeat dosing would raise the cost over time.

  **How we’re dealing with it:** we are not claiming access. We use an **oral capsule format**, which is easier to store and administer than injections or infusions, and we assessed price in the financial assessment. We do not know what it would cost to manufacture or distribute.

  **Why we accept it:** AnTigone is a **proof of concept**, and a student team cannot solve pricing, distribution and regulation.&#x20;
</Card>

<Card>
  <CardTitle image="https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6160/wiki/sustainability/e-web-goal-12.avif" subtitle="Inflicting SDG 12.4: “[…] achieve the environmentally sound management of chemicals and all wastes throughout their life cycle […]” and SDG 6.3: “[…] improve water quality by reducing pollution […]”">
    Environmental & Biocontainment Footprint
  </CardTitle>

  **Target** **12.4** asks for environmentally sound management of chemicals and all wastes, and **6.3** asks for better water quality by reducing pollution.

  A live engineered organism is different from a chemical drug. If it left the gut, patients could shed it into wastewater, and its genes could in principle spread to other bacteria (<Tooltip text="Movement of genetic information between organisms. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4536854/">horizontal gene transfer</Tooltip>), which has been shown for engineered gut bacteria in animal experiments (<Citation reference="wegmann-et-al-2017" />). Wild-type *E. coli* Nissle 1917 stays in the gut for weeks after dosing: in a placebo-controlled study in healthy volunteers, **45 of 48 tested positive during dosing**, and many still did 12 weeks after it stopped (<Citation reference="joeres-nguyen-xuan-et-al-2010" />). Our design relies on redosing, which means continuous production, packaging and, potentially, cold chain. We have not assessed the footprint of manufacturing, and none of our **containment layers** has been tested.

  ---

  **How we’re dealing with it:**

  * Layered design: a hypoxia-responsive gut gate, ΔdapA auxotrophy and a CRISPR kill switch, with chromosomal integration to limit gene transfer. We also considered genetic stability, since mutation can undo containment. (LINK biosafety)
  * We reviewed the EU rules on contained use (<Citation reference="directive-2009-41-ec" />), release (<Citation reference="directive-2001-18-ec" />) and medicinal use (<Citation reference="directive-2001-83-ec" />), and the EMA guideline on environmental risk assessment for medicines containing GMOs (<Citation reference="european-medicines-agency-2006" />). \[Biosafety section]
  * Dr. Chan (LINK) confirmed that a gut gate is only one layer and not a complete solution.

  **Why we accept it:** a live engineered organism is the **core of the idea**. This is the other side of our **SDG 12** claim: if AnTigone replaced hormonal drugs it could reduce **hormone discharge** (see SDG 12 LINK), but it would add a **GMO release risk** that hormones do not have.
</Card>
