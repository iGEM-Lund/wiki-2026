Collaboration was a big part of AnTigone. We worked with other iGEM teams at meet-ups and on our podcast, learned from industry at Ampliqon, and partnered with student organisations, a café, a library and a summer school to reach people outside the lab.

## **iGEM Collaborations**

  Short Summary&#x20;

  Together with iGEM teams from Aarhus, Oslo, Stockholm, Copenhagen, SDU, BKEA, and DTU, we exchanged project ideas and experiences through team pitches and discussions. From hands-on cloning and molecular docking to Human Practices and Wiki development, the workshop gave us valuable skills and inspiration for the season ahead. We also had the chance to meet experts and iGEM Ambassadors, whose advice helped guide our project.

  And of course, we brought iGEM Lund 2026 podcast - [The Sequence](https://open.spotify.com/show/1anr7zYZ3pKdntKfGcVEvz?si=j3_TYq5HQr-aZCCynVjoTQ\&utm_source=copy-link) - to DTU! We recorded an in-person episode with iGEM Oslo and SDU, along with a DTU Special featuring DTU BioBuilders!

      **Human Practices & Wiki Development**

      Beyond the technical workshops, we learned how to make our project more responsible, meaningful, and accessible.

      The Human Practices workshop introduced us to stakeholder analysis and the importance of integrating expert feedback into project decisions through the Design–Build–Test–Learn (DBTL) cycle. We later applied these ideas by engaging with PMOS-related experts and stakeholders, using their feedback to refine our project goals and safety considerations.

      The Wiki workshops taught us how to organize and present our work effectively, from page structure and visual design to GitLab collaboration. These lessons helped us plan our Wiki layout, organize content, and communicate our project's development more clearly.
    </LayoutColumn>
  </Layout2>

European MeetUp-Málaga

2026.07.14 -2026.07.16

Warmly welcomed by iGEM UMA, we spent several exciting days together with iGEM Leiden, KU Leuven, and Bielefeld, sharing ideas, learning from each other, and experiencing the energy of southern Spain.

Throughout the meetup, we met experts from different fields and had the chance to speak directly with an iGEM judge, whose answers to our competition related questions helped us better prepare for the months ahead. UMA also took us beyond the competition, from enjoying the Spanish sun together at the beach to visiting the local Victoria brewery, giving us plenty of time to explore Málaga and build closer connections with the other teams.

At the Mini Jamboree, we practiced presenting our project and learned from other teams and the judges’ feedback. Even more excitingly, iGEM Lund received the **Best Overall Project Award!** 🏆 This gives us even more confidence and motivation for the months ahead.

And of course, we could not miss the chance to bring The Sequence along! 🎙️ We recorded an in person episode with the amazing iGEM Leiden, turning another meetup into a new podcast collaboration.

We also collaborated with iGEM ETH Zurich on their YouTube series, "[Proof It's Not Always Hard](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUqxkL0zQMoQ\&si=vgOH-gsBTfkXX3pg)," featuring a vlog of our Málaga adventure! 🎥 Through this collaboration, we wanted to show another side of iGEM beyond the lab, capturing the friendships, cultural experiences, and unforgettable moments that make this journey so special.

  **Outcome:**

  We were asked two essential questions:&#x20;

  1. How do we know that testosterone is not reduced too much?
  2. How much testosterone can actually be reduced, and how much gets recirculated?

  For the first question, our biosafety work gives a partial answer in design: we propose combining the hypoxia-responsive gut gate with a testosterone-responsive transcription factor (TeiR), so that the enzyme is only produced when testosterone is above a threshold, and expression is expected to decrease as testosterone falls (LINK). This is a proposed design and is untested.&#x20;

  For the second question we have no data. Both can only be answered by experiments and, much later, by clinical studies.
