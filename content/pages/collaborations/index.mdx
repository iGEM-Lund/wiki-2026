Collaboration was a big part of AnTigone. We worked with other iGEM teams at meet-ups and on our podcast, learned from industry at Ampliqon, and partnered with student organisations, a café, a library and a summer school to reach people outside the lab.   &#x20;

  **Let’s Talk Science!**

  The Sequence is a podcast created by iGEM Lund 2026 to connect with iGEM teams from around the world. Through our **World Wide Wednesday** series, we invite different teams to share their projects, experiences, challenges, and journeys in iGEM.

  Beyond scientific discussions, we aim to bring the global iGEM community closer together, exchange ideas, and highlight the people and stories behind the science!

## **iGEM Collaborations**

<Card>
  <CardTitle

    **DTU BioBrick Workshop**&#x20;

  Together with iGEM teams from Aarhus, Oslo, Stockholm, Copenhagen, SDU, BKEA, and DTU, we exchanged project ideas and experiences through team pitches and discussions. From hands-on cloning and molecular docking to Human Practices and Wiki development, the workshop gave us valuable skills and inspiration for the season ahead. We also had the chance to meet experts and iGEM Ambassadors, whose advice helped guide our project.

  And of course, we brought iGEM Lund 2026 podcast - [The Sequence](https://open.spotify.com/show/1anr7zYZ3pKdntKfGcVEvz?si=j3_TYq5HQr-aZCCynVjoTQ\&utm_source=copy-link) - to DTU! We recorded an in-person episode with [iGEM Oslo and SDU](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0DWDCUuViT2wavvZQapTXX?si=jh4DUZIlS5aNa8il4gyOZg\&utm_source=copy-link), along with a [DTU Special](https://open.spotify.com/episode/7EHj4YQg2iEhRVcH1BoW1t?si=UZ0MkE_dT5SGci4ifZz6Pw\&utm_source=copy-link) featuring DTU BioBuilders!

      **Wet Lab & Dry Lab Workshops**

      The workshop gave us the opportunity to strengthen both our experimental and computational skills. In the wet lab, we practiced molecular cloning, including DNA assembly, bacterial transformation, and     plating. Meanwhile, the dry lab introduced us to protein sequence analysis and molecular docking, where we used tools such as BLAST and protein structure databases to identify proteins and explore their interactions with potential substrates.

      Both sessions provided valuable skills that supported our own experimental work, protein modeling, and enzyme optimization.

  **Bringing The Sequence to DTU!**

Through these conversations, we shared our projects, exchanged experiences, and brought the spirit of collaboration beyond the workshop.

      **Mini Jamboree**

      At the Mini Jamboree, we participated in presentation and poster competitions, sharing our project and receiving valuable feedback from judges. They raised important questions about enzyme specificity, testosterone regulation, and biosafety, encouraging us to consider AND-gate circuits, feedback loops, and auxotrophy-based containment.

      Based on their feedback, we placed greater emphasis on biosafety, exploring AND-gate systems, quorum-sensing-based containment, and auxotrophy to improve the safety of our engineered probiotic. More details can be found on our [Safety page](/biosafety).

      We were also proud to receive the Best Overall Project Award! 🏆 This recognition gave us greater confidence and motivation for the months ahead.

      **Outcome:**

      We were asked two essential questions:&#x20;

      1. How do we know that testosterone is not reduced too much?
      2. How much testosterone can actually be reduced, and how much gets recirculated?

      For the first question, our biosafety work gives a partial answer in design: we propose combining the hypoxia-responsive gut gate with a testosterone-responsive transcription factor (TeiR), so that the enzyme is only produced when testosterone is above a threshold, and expression is expected to decrease as testosterone falls (LINK). This is a proposed design and is untested.&#x20;

      For the second question we have no data. Both can only be answered by experiments and, much later, by clinical studies.
    </LayoutColumn>
