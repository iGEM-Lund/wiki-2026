## **Introduction**

We believe that collaboration is the heart of iGEM. Throughout our journey, iGEM Lund 2026 connected with iGEM teams worldwide through workshops, meetups, and podcasts.

From exchanging ideas and learning new skills to receiving valuable feedback that helped shape our project, each collaboration brought new perspectives and opportunities.&#x20;

For us, collaboration is about learning from one another, growing together, and building connections beyond the competition.

    ### **Our Experience at DTU**

    **Wet Lab & Dry Lab Workshops**

    The workshop gave us the opportunity to strengthen both our experimental and computational skills. In the wet lab, we practiced molecular cloning, including DNA assembly, bacterial transformation, and plating. Meanwhile, the dry lab introduced us to protein sequence analysis and molecular docking, where we used tools such as BLAST and protein structure databases to identify proteins and explore their interactions with potential substrates.

    Both sessions provided valuable skills that supported our own experimental work, protein modeling, and enzyme optimization.

    **Human Practices & Wiki Development**

    Beyond the technical workshops, we learned how to make our project more responsible, meaningful, and accessible.

    The Human Practices workshop introduced us to stakeholder analysis and the importance of integrating expert feedback into project decisions through the Design–Build–Test–Learn (DBTL) cycle. We later applied these ideas by engaging with PMOS-related experts and stakeholders, using their feedback to refine our project goals and safety considerations.

    The Wiki workshops taught us how to organize and present our work effectively, from page structure and visual design to GitLab collaboration. These lessons helped us plan our Wiki layout, organize content, and communicate our project's development more clearly.

    **Bringing The Sequence to DTU!**

    One of the highlights was recording The Sequence in person! We welcomed iGEM Oslo and SDU for a joint episode and recorded a DTU Special with DTU BioBuilders. Through these conversations, we shared our projects, exchanged experiences, and brought the spirit of collaboration beyond the workshop.

    A big thank you to DTU BioBuilders and all the participating teams for making this such a memorable experience!

  Warmly welcomed by iGEM UMA, we spent three exciting days with iGEM Leiden, KU Leuven, and Bielefeld, exchanging ideas, learning from experts, and building connections with the wider iGEM community.

    ### **Our Experience in Málaga**

    **Learning from Experts**&#x20;

    Throughout the meetup, we attended talks on synthetic biology, AI in science, bioethics, and inclusive research, gaining new perspectives on scientific innovation and its societal impact.

    We also spoke with an iGEM Ambassador, who shared valuable advice on medal criteria, Wiki preparation, and Human Practices, emphasizing the importance of being reflective, responsible, and responsive. He also offered practical tips for the Grand Jamboree, such as assigning team members to specific topics and preparing for judges' questions. These insights helped us better organize our Wiki work and competition preparation.

    **Mini Jamboree**

    At the Mini Jamboree, we participated in presentation and poster competitions, sharing our project with other teams and receiving valuable feedback from judges.

    The judges raised important questions about enzyme specificity, testosterone regulation, and biosafety, including how to prevent excessive hormone reduction, ensure our probiotic functions effectively in the gut, and address potential risks for individuals with other health conditions. They also suggested exploring AND-gate circuits, feedback loops, and auxotrophy-based containment to improve safety and control.

    These discussions encouraged us to place greater emphasis on biosafety, exploring AND-gate systems, quorum-sensing-based containment, and auxotrophy to improve the safety of our engineered probiotic. [Safety](https://wiki.igemlund.org/safety)

    We were also incredibly proud to receive the **Best Overall Project Award**! This recognition gave us even more confidence and motivation for the months ahead.

    **Collaborations Beyond the Meetup**

    Of course, we brought [The Sequence](https://open.spotify.com/show/1anr7zYZ3pKdntKfGcVEvz?si=j3_TYq5HQr-aZCCynVjoTQ\&utm_source=copy-link) along! 🎙️ We recorded an in-person episode with iGEM Leiden, exchanging project ideas and experiences while strengthening our connection through the podcast.

    We also collaborated with iGEM ETH Zurich on their YouTube series, [Proof It's Not Always Hard](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUqxkL0zQMoQ\&si=vgOH-gsBTfkXX3pg), contributing a vlog of our Málaga adventure to showcase the friendships and cultural experiences that make iGEM special.

    Beyond the competition, we enjoyed the beach together and visited the local Victoria brewery, creating more opportunities to connect with other teams.

    A huge thank you to iGEM UMA and everyone we met for making these three days so memorable!

**Let’s Talk Science!**

The Sequence is a podcast created by iGEM Lund 2026 to connect with iGEM teams from around the world. Through our WorldWide Wednesday series, we invite different teams to share their projects, experiences, challenges, and journeys in iGEM.

Beyond scientific discussions, we aim to bring the global iGEM community closer together, exchange ideas, and highlight the people and stories behind the science!
