An **economic assessment** checks whether a product is feasible outside the lab: **who** would use it, **what** it costs to make and **what** it could sell for.

A complete assessment of **AnTigone**, a genetically modified <Tooltip text="A medicine made of live microorganisms, here a bacterium that works inside the body.">live biotherapeutic</Tooltip> product (GMO-LBP) that lowers androgen levels locally in the gut for a <Tooltip text="Reaching and acting on the whole body, not just the gut.">systemic effect</Tooltip>, would include: market analysis, <Tooltip text="Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental: the outside factors that affect a project.">SWOT/PESTLE</Tooltip>, <Tooltip text="An existing product that a new product is compared against.">comparator evaluation</Tooltip>, regulatory pathway, cost analysis, <Tooltip text="Moving from small laboratory production to large-scale manufacturing.">scale-up</Tooltip> economics, price estimation, business model, equity and accessibility, and funding and risks.

At this stage in the project, our economic assessment focuses on two parts: a **comparator evaluation** (where does AnTigone fit among current PMOS treatments?) and a **price analysis** (what price range is realistic?).

## Comparative Analysis

The comparative analysis is an evaluation of existing products on the market. In this section, the competitive landscape is examined by comparing AnTigone with current PMOS treatments used in Sweden to answer the question: how does AnTigone compare with PMOS treatments already used by patients, and where does it fit among the existing treatment options?

Traditional treatments for PMOS act systemically in the body and include metformin, spironolactone, and combined contraceptive pills, which have a monthly price range of SEK 34–135, while weight-management medications range from SEK 2,600–5,200 <Tooltip text="Without the state paying part of the cost.">without subsidy</Tooltip>. On the other hand, our product acts locally in the gut to induce a systemic effect, lowering testosterone levels throughout the body. In order for our product to compete with the comparators mentioned above, a monthly price of SEK 300–1,000 would be a reasonable target, as it is greater than that of <Tooltip text="Copies of a brand-name medicine sold after its patent expires, usually much cheaper.">generic drugs</Tooltip> and over-the-counter supplements but lower than that of the weight-management drugs. However, the price for our product has not yet been determined, and a preliminary price range will be estimated following further assessment and taking into account production and regulatory considerations. 

## Price Analysis

#### Product Classification and Pricing Approach

The proposed approach for PMOS is an oral capsule containing live, genetically engineered bacteria. As the product is intended for a therapeutic purpose, it will be developed as a live biotherapeutic product (LBT/LBP). Because the bacterial strain is genetically modified, additional safety assessments and <Tooltip text="Genetically modified organism.">GMO</Tooltip>-related regulatory requirements may apply in Sweden and the EU. The final regulatory classification and applicable requirements would need to be confirmed with the [relevant authorities](/biosafety) (<Citation reference="committee-for-medicinal-products-for-human-use-chmp-2006" narrative={false} />; <Citation reference="rouanet-et-al-2020" narrative={false} />).

This price analysis compares four product categories: conventional probiotic supplements, GMO probiotic supplements, non-GMO live biotherapeutic products (LBTs), and genetically modified LBTs (GMO-LBTs). Swedish products are used as the primary market benchmarks, with European and US examples included where relevant. As no directly comparable marketed product for the proposed PMOS treatment has been identified, an illustrative cost model is used to estimate its potential price.

#### Price Progression and Main Cost Drivers

**Conventional Probiotic Supplements**

Conventional probiotics provide a reference point for consumer pricing in Sweden. For example, Apotea and Apoteket list Probi Mjölksyrabakterier Original (80 capsules) at a retail price of SEK 229 (<Citation reference="apotea-n-d-3" narrative={false} />; <Citation reference="apoteket-n-d" narrative={false} />). This price represents what consumers pay for the finished product and does not indicate its manufacturing cost.

The main costs of conventional probiotics include bacterial cultivation, capsule formulation, packaging, quality control and distribution (<Citation reference="fenster-et-al-2019" narrative={false} />). Retail prices of conventional probiotic supplements in Sweden were used as a baseline for comparison. **Genetically Modified Probiotics (GMO Probiotics)**

GMO probiotics are genetically modified live bacteria that are genetically engineered or modified to carry out a particular function. In contrast to conventional probiotics, which contain naturally-occurring live microorganisms, GMO probiotics contain live, genetically altered strains. Because they are genetically modified, GMO probiotics are subject to additional regulations. An environmental risk assessment is required by EU GMO legislation, which is not mandatory for conventional probiotics, to evaluate risks to human health and the environment under Directive 2001/18/EC, and can lead to additional safety measures including <Tooltip text="Built-in measures that stop engineered organisms from surviving or spreading outside their intended place.">biocontainment</Tooltip> (<Citation reference="directive-2001-18-ec" narrative={false} />).

There are currently no GMO probiotics sold in the European market, making a direct price comparison not possible. The nearest reference is ZBiotics in the US, which sells genetically engineered *Bacillus subtilis* in the form of their ZBiotics Sugar-to-Fiber probiotic drink mix, which has a one-time price of USD 80 (≈ €72; conversion at USD 1 = €0.90) for a 30-day supply (<Citation reference="z-biotics-company-n-d" narrative={false} />). Comparing this to the average monthly price of conventional probiotics in Sweden (≈ €12) indicates that a genetically modified probiotic may lead to increased costs of roughly 500%. However, this value is only indicative, as it was determined by comparing a single GMO probiotic on the US market to conventional probiotics in the Swedish market, which are subject to different regulations.

**Live Biotherapeutic Products (LBPs)**

Live biotherapeutic products (LBPs) are products containing live microorganisms intended to prevent, treat or cure a disease. Unlike conventional probiotic supplements, which are generally marketed to support health, LBPs are developed for therapeutic purposes and require evidence of quality, safety and <Tooltip text="How well a treatment works under controlled conditions.">efficacy</Tooltip>. However, they are only on the US market and differ from our proposed product in composition and intended use.

LBPs are priced considerably higher than probiotics, as they are associated with additional production costs. These increased costs mainly come from the presence of living organisms, which need to be produced and stored in specific conditions to survive, as well as strict quality control measures that must be taken, leading to increased costs for the development and performance of strain-specific identity, <Tooltip text="Tests that measure how strong a product is (potency) and how many unwanted microbes it carries (bioburden), here under oxygen-free conditions.">potency, and anaerobic bioburden assays</Tooltip> (<Citation reference="microbiome-therapeutics-innovation-group-and-barberio-2024" narrative={false} />). Additionally, regulatory approval costs, potential patient population, and alternative treatment options need to be considered.

Furthermore, the LBP market is expanding, with a global market size of USD 2.1 billion in 2025, which is projected to grow to USD 7.8 billion by 2034 with a <Tooltip text="Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly growth rate that would take the market from its starting size to its final size.">CAGR</Tooltip> of 15.7% (<Citation reference="sharma-2026" narrative={false} />). As the market grows, increased investment and more established testing and regulatory pathways may arise, simplifying the process and lowering the cost of bringing a new LBP like AnTigone to the market.

**Genetically Modified Live Biotherapeutic Products (GMO-LBPs)**

Genetically modified live biotherapeutic products are therapeutic products containing live microorganisms that have been genetically modified to perform a specific function in the body. Their development may involve additional safety assessments and regulatory requirements associated with genetically modified organisms (GMOs), leading to an increased product cost (<Citation reference="rutter-et-al-2022" narrative={false} />). 

The European Commission's guidance on GMO aspects of <Tooltip text="Medicines being tested in clinical trials, before they are approved for sale.">investigational medicinal products</Tooltip> describes the additional regulatory considerations that can apply to medicinal products containing genetically modified organisms (<Citation reference="directorate-general-for-health-and-food-safety-n-d" narrative={false} />). As of this assessment, no GMO-LBPs have reached the market, as they are still under clinical development.

#### **Estimated Cost Drivers and Pricing of AnTigone**

Most of the cost drivers associated with approved LBPs would also apply to AnTigone, making its cost structure closer to that of a live biotherapeutic product than a conventional probiotic supplement. However, AnTigone may avoid some costs associated with Rebyota and Vowst because it uses a single, defined *E. coli* strain rather than screened human stool, reducing the need for <Tooltip text="Testing stool donors for infections and other health risks before their material is used.">donor screening</Tooltip>. Furthermore, as PMOS is a chronic condition, development costs can be recovered over time through recurring monthly sales to a larger patient population. However, AnTigone would also face additional regulatory costs related to its genetically modified nature, including environmental risk assessment, biocontainment measures and GMO-specific clinical development requirements.

These comparisons help identify the factors that may affect AnTigone's price, but they are not sufficient to determine its actual cost. The proposed monthly price range of SEK 300–1,000 falls between conventional probiotics and approved LBPs, but this price range needs further justification beyond a comparison of existing product prices. Therefore, the proposed range should be treated as a preliminary pricing target rather than a cost-based estimate. To assess whether this range is realistic, ZBiotics provides a useful market reference, with its engineered live bacterial product priced at approximately SEK 790 per month. However, ZBiotics's Sugar-to-Fiber probiotic is not sold as a medicine, whereas AnTigone would require clinical development, <Tooltip text="Good Manufacturing Practice: the quality standards that medicines must be produced under.">GMP-compliant</Tooltip> manufacturing and regulatory approval as a medicinal product. Therefore, ZBiotics's price cannot directly predict AnTigone's costs. It does, however, provide a useful market comparison, suggesting that the upper end of the proposed SEK 300-1,000 monthly range may be more realistic. Nevertheless, the actual price would depend on AnTigone's development and manufacturing costs, which cannot be determined from the available comparisons alone. A more accurate price estimate would require detailed calculations of the daily bacterial dose, production costs and development expenses distributed across the expected patient population. This level of cost modeling is beyond the scope of this assessment but would be a next step.  

## **Conclusion**

Our economic assessment shows that conventional probiotics cost about SEK 50-289 per month, while GMO probiotics cost approximately SEK 790 per month. Approved non-GMO LBTs are considerably more expensive, with treatment courses ranging from USD 9,338 to 19,231 (SEK 92,446 to 190,387 ; conversion at USD 1 = SEK 9.9). As AnTigone is being developed as a therapeutic product, its costs are expected to be closer to those of LBTs than conventional probiotics, with additional expenses potentially arising from GMO-specific regulatory approval and environmental safety assessments. However, estimating its final price remains difficult because GMO-based LBTs are not yet widely available commercially, and their costs are still uncertain. Since AnTigone uses a defined bacterial strain, donor screening costs may not apply. Although GMO-specific requirements may increase initial development costs, increased investment and a more established regulatory pathway can lead to reduced costs in the future as the LBT market continues to grow.
