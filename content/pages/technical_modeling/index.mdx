## Overview&#x20;

**AnTigone** targets hyperandrogenism driven by the gut-androgen axis, a pathway in which colonic bacteria convert steroid precursors into bioactive androgens, particularly testosterone, which are then reabsorbed into circulation. Our dry-lab work asks which engineered enzyme variants are worth building to reduce testosterone, and how those variants would be expected to behave once placed in a living bacterial system.&#x20;

Two enzymes are central to this axis. **AKR1D1** is the human hepatic **Δ4-3-ketosteroid 5β-reductase**, a member of the aldo-keto reductase superfamily, which inactivates androgens and glucocorticoids in the liver and initiates bile acid synthesis (<Citation reference="chen-et-al-2014" />; <Citation reference="faucher-et-al-2008" />).

**ci2350** is a bacterial Δ4-3-ketosteroid 5β-reductase from *Clostridium innocuum*, identified by Arp et al. (2025) as a member of the **Old Yellow Enzyme family (COG1902)**, a flavin-dependent ene-reductase that couples steroid reduction to NADH oxidation under the anaerobic conditions of the gut. It has been shown to reduce progesterone, cortisone and hydrocortisone directly, and *C. innocuum* extracts have been reported to reduce testosterone (<Citation reference="arp-et-al-2025" />, <Citation reference="stokes-et-al-1985" />).

The two enzymes are not homologous: they belong to unrelated families and have converged independently on the same 5β-reduction chemistry, with AKR1D1 acting on Δ4-3-ketosteroids spanning across **C18** to **C21 classes** (<Citation reference="chen-et-al-2011" />), while ci2350 has been shown to reduce **C21 steroids** (<Citation reference="arp-et-al-2025" />).

### **The selectivity problem**

The capacity of both enzymes to accept testosterone as a substrate makes it a candidate for engineering, but wild-type activity alone is insufficient. Effective therapeutic application requires that the enzyme preferentially reduces testosterone over structurally similar steroids, and progesterone is the most demanding comparison. The two molecules share the same core steroid scaffold and differ critically at a single position: testosterone carries a **17β-hydroxyl group**, whereas progesterone bears a **C17 acetyl substituent** (<Citation reference="chen-et-al-2014" />). Any engineered selectivity must therefore be achieved by discriminating between these two chemically subtle substituents on an otherwise identical molecule.

This problem cannot be addressed experimentally without first knowing which variants are structurally worth testing. The sequence space of possible active site mutations is far larger than can be screened at the bench, so a computational filter is needed to narrow it to a tractable set.

Binding affinity alone, however, does not predict how a variant would behave in a bacterial system. Cell growth, enzyme expression levels, and competition between substrates all shape the outcome, and a variant that binds testosterone well in isolation may still perform poorly when those factors are considered.

## Our approach

Our dry lab work addresses both challenges through two integrated strands.

1. **Enzyme specificity:** Perform rational mutagenesis on ci2350 and AKR1D1 that make the enzyme prefer testosterone over progesterone, using molecular docking to guide which residues to change.
2. **Mathematical modelling:** Build a mechanistic model of bacterial growth, enzyme expression and competitive kinetics that uses the docking results to predict how each variant would perform in practice and which parameters most affect the prediction.

Together, these form a connected predictive layer: the first narrows a large design space to a small set of structurally plausible candidates, and the second takes those candidates and asks whether their effect would actually show up in a real bacterial system.

The human AKR1D1 structure was retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (<Citation reference="berman-et-al-2000" />) (PDB entry 3CAV), resolved at 1.90 Å in complex with NADPH (<Citation reference="faucher-et-al-2008" />).

### Structure preparation

All structures were prepared following the standard AutoDock receptor preparation protocol (<Citation reference="eberhardt-et-al-2021" />,<Citation reference="morris-et-al-2009" />). Water molecules and non-essential heteroatoms were removed. NADPH was present in the PDB structure of AKR1D1 and was retained to preserve the biologically relevant binding geometry. For ci2350, no cofactor present, since it is a predicted structure.&#x20;

Hydrogen atoms were added, and protonation states were assigned at physiological pH (7.4), consistent with standard receptor preparation for molecular docking (<Citation reference="morris-et-al-2009" />). Structures were then converted to PDBQT format using MGLTools (prepare\_receptor4.py) (<Citation reference="morris-et-al-2009" />), which assigns Gasteiger partial charges and defines rotatable bonds for the docking software.

### Ligand preparation

Testosterone and progesterone share the four-ring steroid scaffold, with rings labelled A to D from the end bearing the C3 ketone (“The Nomenclature of Steroids,” 1989). They are identical across rings A to C, including the Δ4-3-keto motif on the A ring that is the site of reduction, and differ only at the C17 substituent on the D ring, with 17β-hydroxyl in testosterone and an acetyl group in progesterone (<Ref target="mod-zqfnc" /> and <Ref target="mod-az7ct" />, below).

### Conserved domain analysis

The sequence of each candidate enzyme was submitted to the NCBI Conserved Domain Search (CD Search) (Marchler-Bauer et al., 2017), which compares a query sequence against the Conserved Domain Database (CDD) to identify evolutionarily conserved functional domains and their annotated residue positions. These annotations cover substrate binding, cofactor coordination and catalytic function, and are inferred from structurally and biochemically characterised members of the same protein family. They provided a sequence-level map of the expected functional site and served as the reference for interpreting and validating the predicted active site pockets.

#### **For AKR1D1:**&#x20;

CD Search assigned the query sequence (NP\_005980.1) to the AKR\_AKR1D1-3 family within the aldo-keto reductase superfamily, returning 32 annotated active site positions and a separately annotated catalytic tetrad (<Ref target="tab-vkjtr" />).

### Active site identification

Binding pockets were identified computationally using fpocket v4.0 (Le Guilloux et al., 2009), which detects cavities through Voronoi tessellation and alpha-sphere clustering and returns a ranked list of candidate pockets, each described by geometric and physicochemical descriptors. These predicted pockets were then cross-referenced against residue annotations from NCBI CD Search to identify which pocket best corresponded to the known catalytic and substrate-binding residues of each enzyme family. The pocket showing the greatest agreement with the annotated functional site was selected.

#### **For AKR1D1:**

fpocket identified 22 candidate pockets in the AKR1D1 structure. Cross-referencing against the CD Search annotations showed a single dominant match: Pocket 1 contained 29 of the 32 annotated active site residues and all four members of the catalytic tetrad (<Ref target="tab-ogugt" />). The remaining pockets carried five or fewer annotated residues each, and twelve contained none. Pocket 1 was therefore selected as the functional active site.

### Molecular docking&#x20;

Docking was performed using AutoDock Vina 1.2 (Eberhardt et al., 2021) with an exhaustiveness setting of 32 and an energy range of 4 kcal/mol. Vina combines a stochastic global search for scoring, which samples ligand position, orientation and torsion angles, with local gradient-based optimisation that refines promising candidates into nearby energy minima.

Each ligand was docked independently into each prepared enzyme structure, and the top-ranked pose was recorded along with its predicted binding affinity in kcal/mol. To establish a selectivity baseline, we defined a selectivity score as the difference in predicted affinity between testosterone and progesterone, ΔG(testosterone) – ΔG(progesterone). A negative value indicates that testosterone is preferred by the enzyme. The inherent stochastic variability of the Vina scoring function, approximately ±0.5 kcal/mol, was treated as the noise threshold (Eberhardt et al., 2021), and selectivity differences below this value were not considered meaningful.

After docking, the two enzymes gave comparable absolute affinities but opposite directions of preference.

  | **AKR1D1** | −9.8                       | −9.3                       | +0.5                         | Progesterone   |
  | **ci2350** | −9.1                       | −9.2                       | −0.1                         | Testosterone   |

AKR1D1 bound progesterone 0.5 kcal/mol more tightly than testosterone. Converting this through ΔΔG (|ΔGT – ΔGP|) = RT ln(ratio), with RT = 0.616 kcal/mol at 310 K, gives approximately 2.3-fold selectivity for progesterone. This is consistent with AKR1D1's biological role, where progesterone intermediates are the primary physiological substrates.

### Contact analysis and residue identification

From the top-ranked docking pose, all enzyme residues within 4 Å of each docked ligand were identified. This is a standard cut-off for direct protein–ligand contacts and captures residues involved in van der Waals interactions and short-range electrostatics (Le Guilloux et al., 2009). Residues in proximity to the C17 position were given particular attention, as this is the only position at which the two substrates differ, as discussed previously in the ligand preparation (Chen & Penning, 2014).

#### **For AKR1D1:**

Both substrates share an identical Δ4-3-keto motif on the A ring, so the residues recognising that end cannot distinguish between them. Hence, the six shared contacts, two of which (Y58, E120) are the catalytic tetrad (<Ref target="tab-53c3s" />), confirm that both ligands dock in a catalytically productive orientation.

Progesterone contacts two hydrophobic residues, **V309** and **L311**, which can form a nonpolar surface that accommodates its acetyl group, while testosterone contacts **I57**, which could offer a hydrogen bond for the 17β-hydroxyl. V309 and L311 were targeted against progesterone, while I57 favours testosterone to increase affinity. All four were mutated singly and in combination.

#### **For ci2350:**

As in AKR1D1, the shared contacts cluster around the conserved Δ4-3-keto end of the molecule and include the catalytic residue, indicating that both ligands adopt a productive orientation. Y167 was excluded from mutagenesis, since altering it would be expected to compromise catalysis rather than selectivity.

**A107, R215** and **V263** appear to line the space progesterone’s acetyl group occupies (<Ref target="tab-zkjil" />). R215 is the most interesting of the three: the other two are small hydrophobic residues, but arginine can donate hydrogen bonds, so its contact may be a specific polar interaction rather than a shape fit, which would make it the cleanest target, along with **V59, F71, Q100, M254** and **L259** to increase the specificity towards testosterone.

### Rational mutagenesis

Rational mutagenesis is a protein engineering approach in which specific amino acid substitutions are chosen in advance from structural and mechanistic knowledge of the enzyme, rather than generating them at random and screening (Lutz, 2010).

The residues in <Ref target="tab-53c3s" /> and <Ref target="tab-zkjil" /> were substituted on physicochemical grounds, altering the size, polarity or hydrogen-bonding capacity of the pocket near C17 in a direction consistent with a shift towards testosterone (Alford et al., 2017). Both single-point variants and multi-residue combinations were designed for both enzymes.

#### **For AKR1D1:**

At V309 and L311, the substitutions cover three different mechanisms: serine (S) and threonine (T) shrink the side chain and add polarity to pocket; tyrosine (Y), phenylalanine (F) and tryptophan (W) fill the cavity so the acetyl group has less room; and glutamate (E) and lysine (K) place a charge against a neutral substituent. Comparing them should indicate which property actually governs discrimination towards progesterone.

At I57, all five substitutions replace an aliphatic side chain with one capable of hydrogen bonding, any of which could pair with testosterone’s 17β-hydroxyl. Combinations test whether the two mutation effects are independent. If they are, the selectivity shifts should roughly sum.

  | **Disrupt progesterone C17 contacts**       | V309, L311                    | V309T/S/Y/F/W/E/K, L311S/T/Y/F/E | Remove the hydrophobic surface stabilising the acetyl group       |
  | **Enhance testosterone 17β-OH recognition** | I57                           | I57S/T/N/Q/Y                     | Introduce a hydrogen-bond donor or acceptor near the 17β-hydroxyl |

#### **For ci2350:**

At A107 and V263, the substitutions add volume, narrowing the space progesterone’s acetyl group occupies. For R215, the approach is removal rather than bulk. R215A and R215G strip the side chain back, which would eliminate any hydrogen bond it forms with the acetyl carbonyl.

For testosterone, F71Y adds a hydroxyl to an aromatic ring already in contact with the 17β-hydroxyl, and V59S/T and Q100S/T introduce or shorten a polar side chain so the hydrogen bond geometry can be optimised. Similarly, combination tests were performed.

### Stability screening

Structural stability was evaluated using PyRosetta (Chaudhury et al., 2010) with the ref2015 scoring function (Alford et al., 2017). The ref2015 function returns a single score summing van der Waals packing, hydrogen bonding, electrostatics, implicit solvation and backbone and sidechain torsional strain, with lower, more negative values corresponding to a better-packed and more stable structure.

Local sidechain repacking was performed within a 5.0 Å radius of the mutated position, capturing the first layer of residues most likely to be directly affected by the substitution (Chaudhury et al., 2010). Stability was expressed as the difference between mutant and wild-type (ΔΔG = E(mutant) − E(wild-type)), cancelling the common baseline and isolating the effect of the substitution. A high positive ΔΔG indicates that the mutation destabilises the structure (Alford et al., 2017).

Variants with ΔΔG above +5 REU were excluded; those below this threshold were considered structurally acceptable and advanced to docking (Alford et al., 2017; Kellogg et al., 2011). The threshold is deliberately permissive. It is set to reject severely destabilised variants, such as unresolvable steric clashes or lost buried hydrogen bonds.

24 designed variants were evaluated for stability checks, where 13 passed the +5 REU threshold and were carried forward; 11 were excluded (<Ref target="tab-74y2k" />).

### Mutant docking and candidate prioritisation

All variants passing the stability threshold were subjected to the same docking protocol as the wild-type enzyme, with testosterone and progesterone docked independently and selectivity scores calculated as before. Variants demonstrating a consistent selectivity shift beyond the noise threshold were identified as priority candidates for experimental characterisation.&#x20;
