## Overview&#x20;

**AnTigone** targets hyperandrogenism driven by the gut-androgen axis, a pathway in which colonic bacteria convert steroid precursors into bioactive androgens, particularly testosterone, which are then reabsorbed into circulation. Our dry-lab work asks which engineered enzyme variants are worth building to reduce testosterone, and how those variants would be expected to behave once placed in a living bacterial system.&#x20;

Two enzymes are central to this axis. **AKR1D1** is the human hepatic **Δ4-3-ketosteroid 5β-reductase**, a member of the aldo-keto reductase superfamily, which inactivates androgens and glucocorticoids in the liver and initiates bile acid synthesis (Chen & Penning, 2014; Faucher et al., 2008).

**ci2350** is a bacterial Δ4-3-ketosteroid 5β-reductase from *Clostridium innocuum*, identified by Arp et al. (2025) as a member of the **Old Yellow Enzyme family (COG1902)**, a flavin-dependent ene-reductase that couples steroid reduction to NADH oxidation under the anaerobic conditions of the gut. It has been shown to reduce progesterone, cortisone and hydrocortisone directly, and *C. innocuum* extracts have been reported to reduce testosterone (Arp et al., 2025; Stokes & Hylemon, 1985).

The two enzymes are not homologous: they belong to unrelated families and have converged independently on the same 5β-reduction chemistry, with AKR1D1 acting on Δ4-3-ketosteroids spanning across **C18** to **C21 classes** (Chen et al., 2011), while ci2350 has been shown to reduce **C21 steroids** (Arp et al., 2025).

### **The selectivity problem**

The capacity of both enzymes to accept testosterone as a substrate makes it a candidate for engineering, but wild-type activity alone is insufficient. Effective therapeutic application requires that the enzyme preferentially reduces testosterone over structurally similar steroids, and progesterone is the most demanding comparison. The two molecules share the same core steroid scaffold and differ critically at a single position: testosterone carries a **17β-hydroxyl group**, whereas progesterone bears a **C17 acetyl substituent** (Chen & Penning, 2014). Any engineered selectivity must therefore be achieved by discriminating between these two chemically subtle substituents on an otherwise identical molecule.

This problem cannot be addressed experimentally without first knowing which variants are structurally worth testing. The sequence space of possible active site mutations is far larger than can be screened at the bench, so a computational filter is needed to narrow it to a tractable set.

Binding affinity alone, however, does not predict how a variant would behave in a bacterial system. Cell growth, enzyme expression levels, and competition between substrates all shape the outcome, and a variant that binds testosterone well in isolation may still perform poorly when those factors are considered.

## Our approach

Our dry lab work addresses both challenges through two integrated strands.

1. **Enzyme specificity:** Perform rational mutagenesis on ci2350 and AKR1D1 that make the enzyme prefer testosterone over progesterone, using molecular docking to guide which residues to change.
2. **Mathematical modelling:** Build a mechanistic model of bacterial growth, enzyme expression and competitive kinetics that uses the docking results to predict how each variant would perform in practice and which parameters most affect the prediction.

Together, these form a connected predictive layer: the first narrows a large design space to a small set of structurally plausible candidates, and the second takes those candidates and asks whether their effect would actually show up in a real bacterial system.

### Protein stcructure acquisition

Three-dimensional structures for the two candidate enzymes were obtained through different routes, reflecting the availability of experimental data for each. The human AKR1D1 structure was retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (Berman et al., 2000) (PDB entry 3CAV), resolved at 1.90 Å in complex with NADPH (Faucher et al., 2008).

No experimentally determined structure was available for ci2350. A three-dimensional model was generated using AlphaFold v2.2.0 (Jumper et al., 2021), with the input sequence taken from Arp et al., 2025. Per-residue confidence scores (pLDDT) exceeded 90 (pLDDT > 90, very high) across the model, confirming that the predicted structure met the threshold for structure-based analysis.

### Structure preparation

All structures were prepared following the standard AutoDock receptor preparation protocol (Eberhardt et al., 2021; Morris et al., 2009). Water molecules and non-essential heteroatoms were removed. NADPH was present in the PDB structure of AKR1D1 and was retained to preserve the biologically relevant binding geometry. For ci2350, no cofactor present, since it is a predicted structure.&#x20;

Hydrogen atoms were added, and protonation states were assigned at physiological pH (7.4), consistent with standard receptor preparation for molecular docking (Morris et al., 2009). Structures were then converted to PDBQT format using MGLTools (prepare\_receptor4.py) (Morris et al., 2009), which assigns Gasteiger partial charges and defines rotatable bonds for the docking software.

### Ligand preparation

Testosterone and progesterone share the four-ring steroid scaffold, with rings labelled A to D from the end bearing the C3 ketone (“The Nomenclature of Steroids,” 1989). They are identical across rings A to C, including the Δ4-3-keto motif on the A ring that is the site of reduction, and differ only at the C17 substituent on the D ring, with 17β-hydroxyl in testosterone and an acetyl group in progesterone (Figure 4 - below, link it too?).

Both ligands were retrieved from PubChem (CID 6013 and CID 5994, respectively) (Kim et al., 2025) as three-dimensional SDF structures. Each ligand was processed using MGLTools (prepare\_ligand4.py) (Morris et al., 2009) to assign partial charges and define rotatable bonds, generating PDBQT files for docking input. No additional energy minimisation was performed prior to docking, as AutoDock Vina performs internal conformational sampling through multiple independent search runs during the docking process (Eberhardt et al., 2021).

### Conserved domain analysis

The sequence of each candidate enzyme was submitted to the NCBI Conserved Domain Search (CD Search) (Marchler-Bauer et al., 2017), which compares a query sequence against the Conserved Domain Database (CDD) to identify evolutionarily conserved functional domains and their annotated residue positions. These annotations cover substrate binding, cofactor coordination and catalytic function, and are inferred from structurally and biochemically characterised members of the same protein family. They provided a sequence-level map of the expected functional site and served as the reference for interpreting and validating the predicted active site pockets.
