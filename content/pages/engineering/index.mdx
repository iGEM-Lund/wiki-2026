## Our Engineering Approach&#x20;

Engineering a biological system requires careful experimental evaluation of each individual design. Each design&#x20;

Throughout the development of our project we applied the Design-Test-Build-Learn (DTBL) cycle framework to progressively investigate technical challenges in the different layers of our proposed design.

## Engineering overview

Each cycle generated observation that informed subsequent experiments, alternative design or changes in results interpretation. As if we look at the bigger picture of our project the whole project can be seen as a big DBTL cycle, but inside of the experimental work we can divide and extract DBTL cycles themselves that, once completed helped going one step further the final envisioned design. The main DBTL cycle would describe the evolution of the whole project, while the smaller cycles would show the iterative optimization happening inside each individual phase.

<SliceWheel slices={[{"title":"Design","body":" we describe how the overall experimental workflow/design evolved and was optimized, for example introducing hypoxia-responsive regulation, adding solubility tags, changing construct designs, etc."},{"title":"Build","body":" we describe the optimization and troubleshooting that happened during the building phase (cloning problems, PCR, mutagenesis, expression constructs, etc.), and within this section we could show a smaller/secondary DBTL cycle specifically related to how we improved the building strategy."},{"title":"Test","body":"same idea for the testing phase, where we show the different rounds of assay optimization, controls, changes in conditions, troubleshooting, and then potentially another smaller DBTL cycle around the assay development itself."},{"title":"Learn","body":"bring everything together and explain what we learned from the different iterations and how this informed the next version of the system."}]} cycle />

Our main challenges and small DBTL cycles refers to:

1. **Construct assembly**&#x20;
2. **Enzyme production** and **protein recovery**
3. **Activity characterisation**
4. **Environmental regulations**
