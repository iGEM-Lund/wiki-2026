## Our Engineering Approach&#x20;

Engineering a biological system requires careful experimental evaluation of each individual design. Each design&#x20;

Throughout the development of our project we applied the Design-Test-Build-Learn (DTBL) cycle framework to progressively investigate technical challenges in the different layers of our proposed design.

## Engineering overview

Each cycle generated observation that informed subsequent experiments, alternative design or changes in results interpretation. As if we look at the bigger picture of our project the whole project can be seen as a big DBTL cycle, but inside of the experimental work we can divide and extract DBTL cycles themselves that, once completed helped going one step further the final envisioned design. The main DBTL cycle would describe the evolution of the whole project, while the smaller cycles would show the iterative optimization happening inside each individual phase.

Our main challenges and small DBTL cycles refers to:

1. **Construct Assembly**: establishing a reliable cloning and verification strategy.
2. **Enzyme production** and **protein recovery**: improving recombinant enzyme expression, solubility, and recovery.
3. **Activity characterisation**: developing reliable assays for evaluating testosterone-associated enzymatic activity.
4. **Environmental regulations**: investigating hypoxia-responsive gene expression.

Improvements in cloning allowed the production of alternative enzyme constructs, while expression and activity experiments revealed additional requirements for future optimizations.

## Cycle 1 - Improving the cloning workflow

  The first engineering challenge was assembling correct expression constructs for the candidate enzymes, AKR1D1 and CI2350. Our initial restriction-cloning workflow using pET-11a repeatedly produced low DNA recovery and unsuccessful transformation. In addition, incomplete restriction digestion could not be reliably identified as NdeI and BamHI restriction sites differs only by approximately 40 base pairs (bp).
</Card>

### **D*esign***

Our initial design used restriction digestion of pET-11a with NdeI and BamHI, followed by ligation with the appropriate gene inserts and transformation into *E. coli* TG1.

To investigate the low cloning efficiency, we progressively modified several aspects of the workflow.

1. We compared DNA extraction methods and digestion conditions to improve recovery of the vector backbone.
2. When transformation screening suggested that incomplete digestion might be contributing to unsuccessful cloning, we investigated the performance of the restriction enzymes themselves.
3. We explored an alternative backbone preparation strategy using the pET-BLANK plasmid containing CI2350–MBP.

Unlike the original pET-11a digestion, restriction of this construct was expected to release an approximately 3 kb insert from an approximately 5.4 kb backbone. The difference in fragment sizes would allow the intended backbone to be identified and physically separated from other digestion products by gel electrophoresis.
