## Safety in the lab

All the members who will be performing experiments in the laboratory are given the required safety training by our <Tooltip text="Principal investigator: the senior researcher who leads the lab group.">PI</Tooltip> and completing the safety training was made mandatory before the team started the work in the laboratory. It was also made mandatory to read **General Safety Regulations,** Lund University's own safety handbook (LINK), which has information on how to perform safe lab work at Kemicentrum, the chemistry building inside the university's campus.

When performing experiments inside the lab, the team always wore appropriate clothing such as **lab coats, gloves, safety goggles** and **shoes** and consumption of food and drinks was strictly **prohibited** inside the laboratory areas. The team ensured that the work bench was always cleaned with **70<Tooltip text="v/v means volume per volume: 70 mL of ethanol in every 100 mL of liquid.">% v/v</Tooltip> ethanol** at the end of the lab day and made sure that the equipment used were properly **cleaned** and **switched off** when not in use.

To ensure safety of the team members and to provide guidance, at least **one instructor was present** throughout the lab work and **no lab work was carried out alone.** All **hazardous wastes** were disposed of according to Lund University's regulations (LINK), which are in line with EU and Swedish regulations. The team also ensured that all safety rules were enforced consistently.

**Our laboratory is equipped with:**

* **Emergency Equipment:** Eyewash stations, emergency showers, fire extinguishers, fire blankets and first aid kits.
* **Containment Equipment:** <Tooltip text="The lowest containment level, for work with organisms that are not known to cause disease in healthy adults.">Biosafety Level I</Tooltip> laboratory with sterile work place to perform biological work and <Tooltip text="A ventilated cabinet that draws harmful fumes away from the user.">chemical fume hoods</Tooltip> for preparing and handling hazardous reagents.
* **Spill Control:** Biological and chemical spill kits, absorbent pads.
* **Protective Equipment:** Lab coats, nitrile gloves, safety goggles, face shields (when required).

## Waste Management

All the biological and chemical wastes resulting from our experiments are properly labelled, segregated and disposed of by following Lund University's waste management guidelines (LINK):

* **Biological wastes** are <Tooltip text="Sterilised with pressurised steam.">autoclaved</Tooltip> at 121°C and 1.1 bar for 25 minutes before they are disposed of along with the general wastes to ensure that no microbial contamination is possible.
* **Chemical wastes** are segregated in dedicated chemical waste containers found inside the chemical waste cabinets and are disposed of by the university using **certified hazardous waste contractors**.
* **Glass wastes**, that are not contaminated with any harmful chemicals or biological sources are collected in dedicated bins before they are disposed of along with regular glass wastes.
* **Sharp objects** are disposed of immediately after they are carefully collected and labelled in **puncture-proof containers** and the containers are checked regularly to ensure that they are never overfilled.

## Hazard Identification and Risk Assessments

### Biological Risks:

The team only works with bacterial strains *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3), *Escherichia coli* TG1 and *Escherichia coli* Nissle 1917, which are all considered to be of low risk as they belong to <Tooltip text="The lowest risk class: organisms unlikely to cause human or animal disease.">**Risk Group 1**</Tooltip>. *Escherichia coli* Nissle 1917 is also an active ingredient of a licensed probiotic product. Risk assessments were, however, made for all experiments before being performed in the lab and all <Tooltip text="Work in which DNA from different sources is combined, for example inserting a gene into a plasmid.">**recombinant DNA work**</Tooltip> was conducted in accordance with Lund University's biosafety guidelines (LINK) and <Tooltip text="The EU rules for working with genetically modified microorganisms in closed laboratories and facilities.">**EU Directive 2009/41/EC**</Tooltip> (LINK) on the contained use of genetically modified microorganisms.

### Chemical Risks:

The team also works with toxic chemicals such as <Tooltip text="A reducing agent used in protein sample buffers. It is toxic and has a strong smell.">beta-mercaptoethanol</Tooltip>, methanol, <Tooltip text="A chemical used to wash proteins off a His-tag purification column.">imidazole</Tooltip> and <Tooltip text="A nickel salt used to charge His-tag purification columns.">nickel(II) sulphate</Tooltip>, which are known to be <Tooltip text="Chemicals that can harm fertility or an unborn child.">reproductive toxicants</Tooltip>, and their handling is restricted accordingly. The handling of testosterone also presents <Tooltip text="Contact with the skin or with moist body linings such as the eyes and mouth.">dermal and mucosal exposure</Tooltip> risk from spills or handling of the solid, and is accessed only by dedicated personnel. All chemicals were handled in designated areas, with reference to the <Tooltip text="Documents that describe a chemical's hazards and how to handle it safely.">Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS)</Tooltip>.

## Product Safety

### Current status: Laboratory proof-of-concept only

AnTigone is currently a laboratory research construct and is not administered to humans or animals. No therapeutic product is manufactured, packaged, or distributed as part of this project. All bacterial strains, recombinant DNA constructs, chemicals, and experimental samples are handled within the approved laboratory procedures and disposed of according to Lund University’s waste-management requirements. Any future transition towards animal studies, larger-scale production, or clinical development would require new risk assessments and institutional approval.

## Anticipated Future Risks

If the project is expanded, future work may involve larger culture volumes, higher bacterial concentrations, additional genetic constructs, or procedures that generate <Tooltip text="Fine droplets or particles suspended in the air.">aerosols</Tooltip>, such as <Tooltip text="Breaking open cells with sound waves.">sonication</Tooltip> and <Tooltip text="Spinning samples at high speed to separate their components.">centrifugation</Tooltip>. These changes could increase the risks of spills, aerosol exposure, <Tooltip text="Accidentally transferring material from one sample to another.">cross-contamination</Tooltip>, and biological waste release. Before such work begins, the relevant risk assessments, containment measures, personal protective equipment, and waste procedures would need to be reviewed and updated. Work outside the current laboratory, including animal or clinical studies, would require separate institutional and regulatory approval.
