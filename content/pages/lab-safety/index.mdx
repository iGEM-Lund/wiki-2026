This page shows how we kept our work with genetically modified bacteria and hazardous chemicals, including testosterone, safe: training and rules, equipment, waste handling and risk assessments. All strains, constructs, chemicals and samples were handled according to approved laboratory procedures and disposed of according to Lund University's waste-management requirements. The safety of the bacterium itself (in-host control, environmental biocontainment and GMO regulations) is on the [Biosafety](/biosafety) page.

## Safety in the Lab

All members performing experiments in the laboratory received the required safety training from our <Tooltip text="Principal investigator: the senior researcher who leads the lab group.">PI</Tooltip> (<Ref target="card-9no1e" linkText="Johan Svensson Bonde" />), and completing it was mandatory before the team started work in the laboratory. It was also made mandatory to read **General Safety Regulations,** Lund University's own safety handbook (<Citation reference="kemicentrum-n-d" narrative={false} />), which has information on how to perform safe lab work at Kemicentrum, the chemistry building on the university's campus.

When performing experiments inside the lab, the team always wore appropriate clothing such as **lab coats, gloves, safety goggles** and **closed shoes**, and consumption of food and drinks was strictly **prohibited** inside the laboratory areas. The team ensured that the workbench was always cleaned with **70<Tooltip text="v/v means volume per volume: 70 mL of ethanol in every 100 mL of liquid.">% v/v</Tooltip> ethanol** at the end of the lab day and made sure that the equipment used was properly **cleaned** and **switched off** when not in use.

To ensure the safety of the team members and to provide guidance, at least **one instructor was present** throughout the lab work and **no lab work was carried out alone.** All **hazardous wastes** were disposed of according to Lund University's regulations (<Citation reference="kemicentrum-lund-university-2025" narrative={false} />), which are in line with EU and Swedish regulations. The team also ensured that all safety rules were enforced consistently.

**Our laboratory is equipped with:**

* **Emergency Equipment:** Eyewash stations, emergency showers, fire extinguishers, fire blankets and first aid kits.
* **Containment Equipment:** <Tooltip text="The lowest containment level, for work with organisms that are not known to cause disease in healthy adults.">Biosafety Level 1</Tooltip> laboratory with <Tooltip text="A clean area where work is done without letting unwanted microbes get in, for example near a flame or in a sterile bench.">sterile workspace</Tooltip> to perform biological work and <Tooltip text="A ventilated cabinet that draws harmful fumes away from the user.">chemical fume hoods</Tooltip> for preparing and handling hazardous reagents.
* **Spill Control:** Biological and chemical spill kits, absorbent pads.
* **Protective Equipment:** Lab coats, <Tooltip text="Disposable gloves made of synthetic rubber that resist many chemicals.">nitrile gloves</Tooltip>, safety goggles, face shields (when required).

## Waste Management

All the biological and chemical wastes resulting from our experiments are properly labeled, <Tooltip text="Kept apart by type, so that incompatible wastes are never mixed.">segregated</Tooltip> and disposed of by following Lund University's waste management guidelines (<Citation reference="kemicentrum-lund-university-2025" narrative={false} />):

* **Biological wastes** are <Tooltip text="Sterilized with pressurized steam.">autoclaved</Tooltip> at 121 °C and 1.1 bar for 30 minutes before they are disposed of along with the general wastes to ensure that no microbial contamination is possible.
* **Chemical wastes** are segregated in dedicated chemical waste containers found inside the chemical waste cabinets and are disposed of by the university using **certified hazardous waste contractors**.
* **Glass waste**, which is not contaminated with any harmful chemicals or biological sources, is collected in dedicated bins before being disposed of along with regular glass waste.
* **Sharp objects** are disposed of immediately after they are carefully collected and labeled in **puncture-proof containers** and the containers are checked regularly to ensure that they are never overfilled.

## Hazard Identification and Risk Assessments

### Biological Risks

The team works only with the bacterial strains *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3) and *Escherichia coli* TG1, which are considered low risk as they belong to <Tooltip text="The lowest risk class: organisms unlikely to cause human or animal disease.">**Risk Group 1**</Tooltip>. We still made a risk assessment for every experiment before it was performed, and all <Tooltip text="Work in which DNA from different sources is combined, for example inserting a gene into a plasmid.">**recombinant DNA work**</Tooltip> followed Lund University's guidelines (<Citation reference="kemicentrum-n-d" narrative={false} />) and <Tooltip text="The EU rules for working with genetically modified microorganisms in closed laboratories and facilities.">**EU Directive 2009/41/EC**</Tooltip> on the contained use of genetically modified microorganisms (<Citation reference="directive-2009-41-ec" narrative={false} />).

### Chemical Risks

The team also works with toxic chemicals such as <Tooltip text="A reducing agent used in protein sample buffers. It is toxic and has a strong smell.">beta-mercaptoethanol</Tooltip>, methanol, <Tooltip text="A chemical used to wash proteins off a His-tag purification column.">imidazole</Tooltip> and <Tooltip text="A nickel salt used to charge His-tag purification columns.">nickel(II) sulfate</Tooltip>, which are known to be <Tooltip text="Chemicals that can harm fertility or an unborn child.">reproductive toxicants</Tooltip>, and their handling is restricted accordingly. Testosterone also presents <Tooltip text="Contact with the skin or with moist body linings such as the eyes and mouth.">dermal and mucosal exposure</Tooltip> risks from spills or from handling the solid, and is accessed only by dedicated personnel. All chemicals were handled in designated areas, with reference to the <Tooltip text="Documents that describe a chemical's hazards and how to handle it safely.">Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS)</Tooltip>.
